# Prologue narrative: Twenty-Five Pounds

> **AI-generated draft, not canon.** Written by Claude agents (Anthropic) and not yet reviewed or approved by the project owner. Adapted from the owner's storyboard, docs/storyboards/prologue-twenty-five-pounds.md.

Eight scenes, one Friday evening, District Seven, New London, 2189. The player
is an ordinary tired working person; the game never says what they do for a
living, where they trained, or what they look like (see "Player identity"
below). Nothing here is ominous: the only antagonist is a ticket system.

Items from the owner's storyboard are kept exactly (times, prices, numbers,
lines). Everything else is a **Proposal (owner approval pending)** and is
listed in [`README.md`](README.md). Dialogue text lives in
[`dialogue.md`](dialogue.md), object text in [`interactions.md`](interactions.md),
flags in [`quest-state.md`](quest-state.md).

## Conventions

- **Tone.** Warm, dry, Singaporean-flavoured. Nobody explains 2189. People
  complain about last year's prices, today's commute and tomorrow's
  appointments. The technology is wallpaper.
- **Never punished.** Every choice leads to the same next scene. Choices change
  which lines you hear, never what you can do afterwards or what you receive.
- **Player identity (proposal).** Written in second person, no name, gender,
  employer or background. The apartment is lived-in but generic. A name and
  background can be chosen later without rewriting any scene.
- **Duration (proposal).** About 25 to 35 minutes if the player talks to
  everyone; about 12 minutes on the critical path.

## Scene 1. The Late Train

**Place.** Metropolitan Transit Station, 18:12. **Controls taught.** Walk,
inspect, talk (optional).

**What the player sees.** A tall hall of exposed pipework and wet concrete,
crowded shoulder to shoulder at the end of a working week. Hi-vis jackets and
pressed office shirts stand in the same queue. Overhead, a display board
reads *Estuary Line: ON TIME* in green, directly above a smaller line that
reads *Delayed 14 min* in amber. Beside it, a banner from the transit
authority: **98.7 % PUNCTUALITY**. Between the platform doors, glossy
advertisements for household robots, with a smiling robot carrying a tray, and
the price in large friendly numerals: **£480,000**. Under it, in the small
print nobody reads: *Financing available. Robot not included in your
tenancy.*

**What the player hears.** The hum of the station. Pre-recorded announcements,
politely cheerful. Someone's phone playing a drama serial too loudly. A
vending machine that beeps at no one.

**What the player does.** The scene opens with the player at the platform edge
and the board ahead. Walking up to the board, the banner and the robot
advertisement gives inspection text (see
[`interactions.md`](interactions.md), S1-01 to S1-06). Three passengers
nearby will talk if approached (S1 dialogue). None is required.

**The train arrives late.** The announcement thanks passengers for their
patience and repeats the 98.7 % figure. The doors open and the platform
crowd surges. The player is carried in, whichever direction they were facing.

**On the train.** The carriage is full. A recorded voice asks passengers to
"please move towards the centre of the carriage." A passenger challenges it
aloud, asking the announcement to show where the space is, and the carriage
good-naturedly proves the point: everyone shuffles inwards, nobody gets
anywhere. The player may join the shuffle or watch. The announcement thanks
everyone for their cooperation.

**Through the window.** As the train leaves the station the player can look
out. In order: the low, grey, windowless halls of an LFTR facility behind
fences, with insulated heat-network pipes running alongside the track, then
the pipes branching into older terraced blocks with laundry on balconies, then
the lights of the city. Nobody on the train looks. (This is a view, not an
exposition: the player can inspect S1-08 to S1-10 for one line each.)

**Worldbuilding carried, not stated.** Cheap nuclear power has not made trains
punctual, carriages roomy, robots affordable or announcements honest.

**Transition to Scene 2.** About half way along, the player's phone buzzes with
a notification: *Tenant Services: your case needs attention.* The carriage
swings round a bend and the clock reads 18:41.

## Scene 2. The Maintenance AI

**Place.** Train carriage, 18:41. **Controls taught.** Dialogue choices.

**Trigger.** The notification from Scene 1. The player opens it (or ignores
it; it opens itself on the next buzz, because the system is persistent, not
because the player is punished).

**What happens.** A tenant-services assistant, text on the phone, appears in a
chat panel over the carriage view. Beneath its cheerful avatar the case
history shows: *Air-conditioning unit: cooling inadequate. Reported 4 days
ago.* The player has, in advance, attached a photograph of a cheap window
thermometer reading about 35 °C.

The conversation has three rounds, and each round ends in the AI closing the
complaint.

1. The player reports the symptom. The AI thanks them for **confirming that
   the compressor is operating**, which it counts as proof the unit works,
   and offers to close the case.
2. The player says the room is about 35 °C. The AI states that **temperatures
   below 36 °C are acceptable** under the service standard, and offers to
   close the case.
3. The player asks for a technician or a human. The AI explains that
   technician visits are available for "non-compliant" cases and closes the
   case.

Choices (patient, firm, wry, or asking for a human) change the AI's wording
and the player's own lines. They never change the outcome, and the player
loses nothing by choosing any of them.

**The joke.** The AI sincerely thanks the player for the best news of its day:
that the compressor is working. It is not being cruel; it is being exactly
correct about the wrong thing.

**Passengers.** Nearby passengers may overhear. One sympathises, one says
theirs has been four months. (Optional, see dialogue.)

**The survey.** The AI offers a customer satisfaction survey. The player can
take it or dismiss it; both are fine.

**Transition to Scene 3.** The chat ends with the case showing *Resolved*. The
train slows. The carriage empties at the stop for Mercer Lane. The clock jumps
to 19:26. (Proposal: between 18:41 and 19:26 the player walks from the station
to Mercer Lane; this is a fade, not a playable stretch.)

---

## Scene 3. The Last Honest Boiler

**Place.** Mrs Tan's noodle shop, Mercer Lane, 19:26. **Controls taught.**
Buying, small choices.

**What the player sees.** A narrow lane under layered neon, with industrial
piping threaded along the shop fronts and steam from a district vent. Mrs
Tan's shop: a few tables, a counter, a hand-painted menu with the prices
crossed out and rewritten more than once. Warm light, crowded, loud. At the
back, behind the counter, an old stockpot boiler on the district heat feed, the
only machine in the shop with no screen. A hand-lettered card is taped to it:
*Boiler: honest.* (Proposal: the title's "last honest boiler" is this object.)

**The argument.** A customer at the counter is complaining. Mee pok was **£22**
last year; today the board says **£25**. Mrs Tan says her supplier raised the
price. The customer cites the official inflation statistics. Mrs Tan answers:
**"Alamak, you go fly kite lah!"** The customer, undeterred, remarks that
electricity is supposedly nearly free these days, so why is anything dearer?
Mrs Tan suggests that people who complain about cheap nuclear electricity
should go home and eat electricity.

The player can listen, step in with a view, or say nothing (see dialogue).

**Ordering.** The player orders **mee pok, dry**, at £25, paid at the counter.
Optional: chilli or no chilli, a drink (proposal), a seat (proposal, the
shop offers the only free stool near the boiler). Mrs Tan looks at the
player, notices how tired they are, and without comment puts **an extra
fishball** in the bowl. Whatever the player said, they receive it.

**Transition to Scene 4.** The player takes the bowl to a table. Clock 19:38.

---

## Scene 4. Conversations Over Dinner

**Place.** Noodle shop, 19:38. **Controls taught.** Overhearing, optional
conversation.

**What the player sees and hears.** The shop at full tilt. Three small
conversations the player can drift between while eating.

- **Two maintenance workers** in grey coveralls compare notes on their
  government-funded retraining, which moves them from thorium-breeding HTGR
  work to LFTR systems. They grumble about course fees and subsidies, who
  qualifies, and whose subsidy came through. One of them delivers the day's
  best line: **"Last time machine stupid, can call human. Now machine stupid,
  human tell you ask machine."**
- **An elderly man and his granddaughter** share a table. He tells her about
  working from paper equipment manuals, thick binders, annotated in pencil.
  She thinks manual operation sounds dangerous. He thinks she is probably right
  and also that the binders never needed a software update.
- **Mrs Tan**, between orders, comes to clear a bowl and asks the player how
  the day was. When the player mentions the broken aircon, she asks about it
  properly. She says she knows someone and that he is coming about her
  freezer.

Nobody addresses the player as an outsider. Nobody explains what an HTGR or a
LFTR is. The player, if curious, can infer it from how the workers talk.

**Transition to Scene 5.** The shop door chimes. It is 20:04.

---

## Scene 5. The Refrigeration Technician

**Place.** Noodle shop, 20:04. **Characters.** Ah Seng.

**What happens.** Ah Seng comes in with a battered toolbag, a set of pressure
gauges and a head torch pushed back on his forehead. Mrs Tan's walk-in freezer
at the back has been making a noise all week. Ah Seng listens before he
touches anything, opens the panel, and spins the evaporator fan by hand. He
diagnoses a **failing fan bearing**: rough, noisy, a little hot. The player can
come along and hold the torch (optional) and hear the sound for themselves.

**The player's aircon.** The player asks about their own unit. Ah Seng says
there are several things it could be and he can't say without looking at it;
the first thing to do is an actual inspection. He explains, in a sentence or
two, that "compressor running" and "cooling" are different questions. He does
**not** diagnose the fault. (Storyboard: preserve the later investigation.)

**The fee.** Ah Seng quotes **£40**. Mrs Tan negotiates. They settle on
**£35**. Mrs Tan insists she was being generous. Afterwards she gives him
noodles, which was always part of the deal. (Proposal: the player may witness
or even referee the negotiation; both Mrs Tan and Ah Seng are happy
whichever way.)

**HVAC Maintenance skill (introduction).** If the player asked Ah Seng
anything about their aircon, a quiet notice appears: *You are curious about
cooling systems. HVAC Maintenance: introduced.* No levels, no points,
nothing to grind (proposal for the form; the storyboard only says
"introduce a potential HVAC Maintenance skill").

**Ah Seng as a recurring character.** He leaves the shop at the end of the
scene and says where he'll be on Saturday morning if the player wants a hand
(proposal, see the Saturday hook below).

**Transition to Scene 6.** The player finishes the bowl. Clock jumps to 21:47.

---

## Scene 6. The Journey Home

**Place.** Residential district, 21:47. **Controls taught.** Short traversal.

**What the player sees.** A residential district at night, light rain starting
(proposal: the rain begins here so the montage's rain is not a surprise). The
escalator up from the street-level plaza to the housing deck has a barrier
across it and a handwritten sign. The apartment block's lift has a laminated
notice: *Overdue maintenance. We apologise for the inconvenience.* The notice
lists the date the maintenance was due. It is a long time ago.

**What the player does.** Climbs **seven flights of stairs**. A counter on the
landing walls ticks the floors. Each landing has one small detail: a
bicycle, a plant in a paint tin, someone's drying laundry, a cat. A neighbour
on the fourth landing (proposal) can chat.

**Tone.** Comic frustration. The player's stamina does not matter; nothing is
at risk; the animation gets slower and the sighs get longer.

**Transition to Scene 7.** The player reaches the front door. Door unlocks to
the player's palm or key; the door sticks a little. Clock about 22:00.

---

## Scene 7. The Apartment

**Place.** Player's apartment, about 22:00.

**What the player sees.** One compact room with a kitchenette, a bed-sofa, a
window and a wall-mounted air-conditioning unit that hums reassuringly. The
air is thick. A thermostat panel next to the unit shows **22 °C** in confident
green. The cheap window thermometer on the sill shows about **35 °C**. A
terminal on the desk shows the tenant-services portal.

**What the player does.** The main beat is a second contact with the
maintenance AI. The ticket is marked *Resolved*. The AI refuses to escalate
a resolved ticket, offers another customer satisfaction survey, and
cheerfully reminds the player of the 36 °C standard.

Inspectable objects: the aircon unit, the thermostat, the thermometer, the
window, the maintenance portal (terminal). See
[`interactions.md`](interactions.md).

**The terminal.** The player may contemplate smashing it. A thought bubble
runs the arithmetic: **£1,850** to replace. The player lowers their hand. (The
terminal is not breakable in the game; the thought is the whole joke.)

**The window.** The player opens it for air and is hit by the noise and warm
exhaust of the district's industrial ventilation fans, which turn out to be
louder than the heat. The player can close it again. Both states are fine.

**Saturday hook.** Looking at the aircon, the player's journal updates with
an optional entry: *Saturday: find out what is wrong with the aircon.* It is
optional, not a task with a timer. (Quest details are in
[`quest-state.md`](quest-state.md).)

**Transition to Scene 8.** When the player is ready, they sit on the bed-sofa.
The room goes quiet. Clock 22:03.

---

## Scene 8. Closing Montage

**Place.** Apartment and New London skyline, 22:03. **Controls.** None.

**Visual.** Rain on the window. The camera lifts off the apartment and rises
over the city: the reactors, quietly operating; factories and hospitals with
their lights on; wealthy towers glittering above the weather; crowded
residential blocks beneath. Then back down through the rain to a window with
a small, warm light.

**Narration (storyboard beats, voiced as plain statements, in order).**

- The reactors operate.
- Factories and hospitals continue functioning.
- AI systems process enormous amounts of information.
- Wealthy households enjoy robotic servants.
- The player still has a broken aircon.

**Final line.** **"Alamak."**

Cut to black.

**Title reveal.** RETRIBUTION AND APPEALS

(There is no stinger, no teaser, no hook beyond the title. The prologue does
not need a moral.)
