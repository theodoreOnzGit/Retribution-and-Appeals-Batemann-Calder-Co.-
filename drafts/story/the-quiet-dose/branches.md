> Nothing below ends the game. Every wrong turn costs something (time,
> access, relationships, or harm to other people) and leaves a route back to
> the truth. Relationship effects use the three axes of brief §11

## 1. How accusations work

The player can present a theory to six audiences. Each listens to different
evidence and does different things:

| Audience | What it wants | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Mara** | The truth about her father, and to be included | Grant access to his records, vault and chart; withdraw or pursue the appeal |
| **Inspector Okafor** (police) | A named suspect and a physical link | Arrest, search, survey; he cannot be argued out of an arrest quickly |
| **Coroner's Appeal Bench** | Evidence that the finding is unsafe, with sound chain of custody | Preservation, disclosure and survey orders; quash the finding; refer to police |
| **The press** (the *Reach Gazette*) | A story | Public pressure; makes institutions defensive |
| **Vale** | Honesty | Help, resources, or distance |
| **The accused** | (varies) | Confess, deny, retaliate, or help |

## 2. Wrong accusations and their consequences

### 2.1 Accusing Dr Voss (to Okafor)

- **What happens:** Okafor arrests her for aiding the unauthorised vault
  entry, which is a real offence. Her lab is sealed for five game days.
- **Cost:** any samples held at her lab are frozen; if the split samples had
  not already gone to the reference lab (E14), the player must apply to the
  Bench to unseal them, and B&C's lawyers will argue the chain of custody is
  broken. The Vale Foundation suspends her grant pending inquiry.
- **Relationships:** Mara Affinity ↑ at first (she resents Voss), then Trust ↓
  when Voss is cleared. Voss Affinity ↓↓ for the rest of the game. Voss
  Respect recovers if the player apologises with the evidence that cleared
  her. Vale Respect ↓ unless the player tells him plainly they were wrong.
- **Route back:** E14 and E18 clear her; the lab reopens; she helps, coldly.
- **Lasting change:** Voss refuses future favours without a written request.

### 2.2 Accusing Gideon Hale (to the Bench or the press)

- **What happens:** B&C suspends him. The investigation exposes E28: about
  three hundred workers' dose records are back-corrected.
- **Good done:** the line crews gain correct records; eleven workers become
  eligible for dose-constraint reviews. Crane Trust ↑. A real wrong is
  righted, though not the one the player was chasing.
- **Cost:** Hale stops cooperating. E05 (the survey map) now needs a Bench
  order: three days' delay. B&C closes ranks; E20 cannot come through B&C
  procurement.
- **Relationships:** Hale Affinity ↓↓, Respect ↑ if the player later says
  publicly that he was not the killer.
- **Route back:** E05 by order; E16 and E19 point away from Erith Reach.

### 2.3 Accusing Batemann & Calder (to the press)

- **What happens:** the *Reach Gazette* runs it. B&C's lawyers send letters;
  the player's site access to Erith Reach is withdrawn.
- **Good done:** public pressure makes the Bench grant orders a day faster
  for the rest of the case. The Vault 3 inventory question becomes public,
  which Elias wanted.
- **Cost:** B&C's security contractor watches Mara openly; Mara Trust ↓ if she
  was not consulted. Celia Strand withdraws the "goodwill payment".
- **Route back:** the killing is not B&C's; E16 and E19 lead elsewhere. The
  cover-up thread is real and continues into later cases.

### 2.4 Accusing Tobias Crane (to Okafor)

- **What happens:** Crane is arrested. The Line Crews' Union walks out on the
  Heatline for a night. **District heat in Belvedere Flats is cut for that
  night** in midwinter; families with the least are hit hardest. (This is the
  brief's central question in miniature: justice pursued carelessly harms
  people who depend on the system.)
- **Cost:** Crane's depot contacts close; E20 now needs a Bench order. The
  Reach families distrust the player for the rest of the game (lasting
  Affinity ↓ with the Reach Families Association as a group).
- **Route back:** E18 and E15 clear him; he is released; if the player
  apologises in person in Belvedere, he tells the car-park story (R4) and
  some trust returns.

### 2.5 Accepting the suicide theory (T6)

- **What happens:** the Bench substitutes a finding of suicide. The
  compensation is void. Mara is devastated; Julian comforts her.
- **Cost:** Mara Affinity ↓↓ if the player argued for it; Trust ↓.
- **Route back:** the Bench allows a second appeal on fresh evidence. E17,
  E18 and E19 found later reopen the case. Julian's confirmation hearing
  goes ahead meanwhile (see §4).

### 2.6 Accusing Vale

- **What happens:** Vale is courteous, gives a full account of his
  movements, and keeps funding Voss's lab. He says something true and
  uncomfortable (R6).
- **Relationships:** Vale Affinity ↓, Trust ↓, **Respect ↑ if the player's
  reasoning was careful, ↓ if it was reckless.** This is the first place the
  player learns that the axes move separately (brief §11).
- **Lasting change:** Vale becomes a little more guarded in later cases; he
  remembers.

### 2.7 Accusing Julian too early (before E16 to E20)

- **What happens:** Julian is wounded and dignified. Mara is furious:
  "He's the only family I have left." Julian's lawyers, who appear with
  surprising speed (the Compact's shield, `vale-and-the-compact.md` §6.4),
  obtain an order limiting the player's direct access to Directorate records.
  Julian urges Mara to withdraw the appeal "before this destroys you."
- **Cost:** Mara Affinity ↓↓, Trust ↓↓; E19 must come through a public-records
  request in Mara's name, which she will only make once the player has shown
  her E16.
- **Route back:** Voss and the evidence. A correct but premature accusation
  is still correct; the price is paid in Mara's trust, which must be rebuilt.

| Ending | What the player has proved | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **E-α. The finding stands** | Nothing, or chose not to appeal | Mara is paid. Julian becomes Chief Inspector and is warm to the player. The Vault 3 measurements are lost unless Voss keeps them. In a later case, a second death on the Reach can reopen everything. |
| **E-β. The finding is quashed** | Questions 1 to 3 of the solution chain: non-occupational internal intake | New finding: death from internal radioactive contamination of non-occupational origin, manner undetermined; referred to police. Compensation void. Mara has the truth about the cause but not the person. The case stays open as a thread. |
| **E-γ. Julian is charged** | Questions 1 to 5 | Okafor arrests him. The Crown prosecution's success is not guaranteed (the defence attacks chain of custody and motive `[src: nist-coc, kassin2013]`). His confirmation is withdrawn. |
| **E-δ. Julian is charged and 2158 is exposed** | All six questions, with E25 and E27 made public | As E-γ, and the Directorate reopens Belvedere Flats. Elias's own complicity becomes public. Some Reach families are paid; the decommissioning settlement is renegotiated; the Belvedere redevelopment stalls for a year. Vale responds (open, `vale-and-the-compact.md` §8). |

### 4.1 Two choices inside the endings

1. **The compensation (Mara's choice, the player advises).** Appealing
   suspends the payment; a non-occupational finding voids it. A civil claim
   against Julian, or a share of a reopened Belvedere settlement, might
   replace it years later, or might not. The player can argue either way;
   Mara decides, and her Affinity and Trust with the player respond to how
   honestly the stakes were put to her, not to which way she chooses.
2. **Elias's name (Mara's choice).** Exposing 2158 exposes her father's
   signature. Elias wanted it exposed (E25). Mara may choose to keep it
   sealed and let Julian be tried for the killing alone. The canon has
   precedents for a detective accepting private judgement when the law would
   do more harm (*The Abbey Grange*,
   text:the-return-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L11455-L11471; *The Devil's Foot*,
   text:his-last-bow-06-the-devils-foot.txt#L1050-L1058). **This proposal
   does not let the player privately excuse a murder**; the private choice is
   only about the 31-year-old record, and it has a cost: without it the Reach
   families get nothing.

### 4.2 The confrontation with Julian

Available once Questions 1 to 5 are answered. How it goes depends on how
the player treats him and what the player holds:

| Player holds | Player's manner | Julian's response |
|---|---|---|
| E25 (Elias's statement) | Respectful | He reads the diary line about the 14th, breaks, and confesses to the 2158 falsification and, after a long silence, to the killing: "He was going to take thirty years of my life back to Woolwich on the 14th and lay it on a desk." Whether he says he meant Elias to die stays ambiguous unless the player asks directly. |
| E25 | Contemptuous | He confesses to 2158 only and asks for his lawyer. |
| No E25 | Any | He denies everything, calmly, and points out every gap in the evidence (a useful recap for the player). |

Julian never says that anyone ordered him. If asked about "the people who
leaned on you in '58", he says: "Nobody asked me to do anything. Nobody ever
has to."

## 5. Relationship outcomes worth tracking for later cases

- **Mara:** potential long-term ally (she reads AI logs); her view of the
  player is set by honesty, not by success.
- **Voss:** the player's scientific mentor if treated well; a cold
  professional if accused.
- **Hale:** an enemy or, if treated fairly, a reluctant inside source at B&C.
- **Crane and the Reach families:** a community that remembers.
- **Okafor:** learns to listen to the player if the player was right without
  humiliating him (the *Silver Blaze* lesson: Gregory listens once his
  attention "had been keenly aroused",
  text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L834-L836).
- **Vale:** whatever the player did, he noticed.
