## Relationships

Watson, back from Afghanistan with a jezail wound from Maiwand (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L74`), meets Holmes through Stamford at Bart's (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L109`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L237`), and they take rooms at 221B Baker Street (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L386`). Jefferson Hope was to have married Lucy Ferrier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4176`). ~~Drebber and Stangerson were two of the "four principal Elders" of the settlement (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2961`).~~ **corrected 2026-10-17** The four principal Elders under Young were Stangerson, Kemball, Johnston and Drebber (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2960-L2962`); the victims are their *sons*. Joseph Stangerson is Elder Stangerson's son, who rode in the waggon that carried the child Lucy (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2949-L2952`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3395-L3398`), and Enoch Drebber is "the son of Elder Drebber" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3395`). In London, Stangerson is Drebber's private secretary (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2042-L2047`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1806-L1807`). Lucy was not Ferrier's child: she was the only other survivor of a party of twenty-one, and he adopted her (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2834-L2842`). Drebber and Stangerson lodged with Madame Charpentier, a widow, in Torquay Terrace, Camberwell; her son Arthur is a sub-lieutenant in the navy and her daughter is Alice (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1804-L1806`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1898`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2035-L2047`).

## Actual chronology

1. Part II: John Ferrier and the child Lucy, dying on the Great Alkali Plain, are rescued by the Mormon emigrants on condition they join the faith (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2834-L2910`).
2. Lucy grows up and falls in love with Jefferson Hope (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3022-L3138`). Brigham Young gives her a month to choose between the sons of Stangerson and Drebber (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3292-L3301`); daily warnings count down the days (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3511`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3547`).
3. Hope leads the Ferriers out by night (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3604-L3724`). While he hunts for food, Ferrier is killed and Lucy taken (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3854-L3876`); the grave is dated August 4th, 1860 (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3868-L3870`). It was Stangerson who shot her father (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3943-L3944`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4383`); she is married to Drebber and dies within a month; Hope takes the ring from her finger (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3934`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3963-L3975`).
4. Hope pursues Drebber and Stangerson for years, to Cleveland, St. Petersburg, Paris and Copenhagen, then London, where he becomes a cabman (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4040-L4060`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4190`).
5. On the night of 3 March (they were seen at Euston about 8.30 on "the third" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2191-L2192`)) Drebber misses the Liverpool train, goes back to Torquay Terrace drunk, tries to carry off Alice Charpentier, and is thrown out by Arthur with a cudgel (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2045-L2066`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4225-L4290`). He hails Hope's cab for Halliday's Private Hotel. Hope has a duplicate key, moulded from one dropped in his cab, to an empty house in the Brixton Road (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4256-L4262`). He drives Drebber to Lauriston Gardens and makes him choose between two pills, one poisoned with an alkaloid stolen at York College; Drebber dies, and Hope writes RACHE in his own blood (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4308-L4317`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4371-L4412`).
6. He goes back for the dropped ring and is taken for a drunk by Constable Rance (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4415-L4420`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1188`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1218`).
7. Stangerson, at Halliday's Private Hotel, refuses the pills and is stabbed (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2209`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4436-L4438`).
8. Holmes advertises the ring (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1568-L1570`); an "old woman" collects it (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1669-L1758`). Gregson arrests Arthur Charpentier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2108-L2126`) after Madame Charpentier, pressed, cannot say when her son came home (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2076-L2105`).
9. Holmes tests the pills on a terrier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2320-L2378`), and Wiggins brings the cabman up to Baker Street, where Holmes handcuffs him (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2452-L2483`).
10. Hope dies of his aneurism the night after his capture (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4488-L4490`); the *Echo* credits Lestrade and Gregson (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4657-L4673`).

## Apparent explanation

A political murder by German revolutionaries, as the press reads RACHE (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1789-L1793`), or a crime of the Charpentier family (Gregson) or of Stangerson (Lestrade) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2409-L2410`).

## Culprit and motive

Jefferson Hope, avenging John and Lucy Ferrier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4176-L4179`).

## Evidence and investigative techniques

- The blood reagent: "an infallible test for blood stains" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L234-L253`).
- Lauriston Gardens: cab ruts, stride length, square-toed boots, Trichinopoly cigar, florid face from the nosebleed (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1198`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1234-L1250`).
- RACHE is not German work: the A is printed "after the German fashion", which a real German would not do (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1209`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1300-L1302`).
- The ring as bait (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1482-L1483`).
- The telegram to Cleveland. Holmes asks only about Drebber's marriage; the answer is that Drebber had applied for protection against "an old rival in love, named Jefferson Hope", now in Europe (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4612-L4619`). Gregson's telegram had asked nothing in particular (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1077-L1090`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4596-L4599`). The telegram in Stangerson's pocket, "J. H. is in Europe", fits it (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2279-L2282`).
- The cabman: the horse had wandered with no one in charge of it, so the driver was inside the house; and a man following another through London would turn cabman (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4621-L4632`). The Irregulars are sent to every cab proprietor (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4639-L4641`).
- The blood on the floor followed the murderer's footsteps, so it was his own nosebleed, not a wound (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4604-L4610`).
- The pills and the terrier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2329-L2378`).
- Reasoning backwards from a result to its causes (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4521-L4536`).

## Red herrings and misdirection

RACHE and the woman's wedding ring (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1036`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2128`). The Charpentiers' confused evidence (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1956-L2076`): Madame Charpentier first denies seeing Drebber again (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1976-L1981`), and Arthur has no address for the "old shipmate" of his alibi (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2134-L2139`). Lestrade's "Rachel" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1134-L1139`). The first half-pill that does nothing to the terrier (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2344-L2366`).

## Twists

The killer is the cabman everyone overlooked; the story then jumps to Utah for the "why". The second murder confirms Holmes rather than defeats him: the pill box he had predicted is found by Stangerson's body (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2289-L2293`). Holmes is beaten once: the "old woman" who collects the ring was "a young man, and an active one", who vanished from a moving cab (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1736-L1766`), and Hope refuses to name him (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4461-L4469`). The accomplice is never identified.

## Ethical questions

- Hope: "There is no murder... Who talks of murdering a mad dog?" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4378`). He leaves the outcome to the pills, "justice upon the earth, or... chance" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4388-L4389`).
- He dies before a trial: "A higher Judge" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4488`).
- The official force takes the credit (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4672-L4673`).
- Doyle's portrait of the Mormon settlers ("Danite Band, or the Avenging Angels" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L3222-L3223`)) is hostile, sensational fiction about a real religious community. A game should not reuse it as a portrait of any real group.

## Textual notes

- The text spells the substance "hoemoglobin"; a transcriber's footnote says it should be haemoglobin (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L234`) (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L4711`).
- Holmes dismisses Dupin and Lecoq (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L684-L702`).
- Dates disagree inside the text: the *Standard* says Drebber and Stangerson left the boarding-house on "Tuesday, the 4th inst." (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L1807-L1808`), while Lestrade has them at Euston "on the evening of the third" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L2191-L2192`), and Watson dates the morning of Gregson's letter to "the 4th of March" (`text:a-study-in-scarlet.txt#L567`).

## Proposal (not canon): nuclear-cyberpunk adaptation

A cab or freight-drone operator, dying of a chronic condition, hunts two officials of a closed company town (a mining or thorium-extraction colony with its own law) who forced his fiancée into a contract marriage and killed her father. The player meets the case as an apparently political killing with a planted slogan. The second half moves to the colony, which lets the game show a self-governing institution with impunity. The adaptation must invent its own community and not echo Doyle's caricature of a real faith.
