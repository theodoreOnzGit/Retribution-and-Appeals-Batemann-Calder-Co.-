## Relationships

Stapleton is the son of Rodger Baskerville, Sir Charles's younger brother, who fled to South America (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6530-L6536`). He married Beryl Garcia of Costa Rica, ran a school in Yorkshire as Vandeleur, and came to Dartmoor as Stapleton, with his wife as his "sister" (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6534-L6554`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5937-L5940`). Eliza Barrymore is Selden's elder sister (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L3786-L3794`). Laura Lyons is Frankland's daughter (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L4325-L4329`).

## Actual chronology

1. The legend: Hugo Baskerville, hunting a girl across the moor, is killed by a hound (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L389-L494`); the manuscript is dated 1742 (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L317`).
2. Stapleton, learning that only two lives stand between him and the estate, settles near the Hall, buys a savage dog in Fulham Road, hides it in the Grimpen Mire, and treats it with phosphorus (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6551-L6585`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6262-L6268`).
3. He gets Laura Lyons, whom he has promised to marry, to write asking Sir Charles to meet her at the gate at ten; he then stops her going (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L4227-L4229`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L4693-L4698`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6597-L6623`).
4. Sir Charles waits at the gate; the hound is loosed; he runs and dies of heart failure (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L563-L601`). Mortimer sees the footprints of a gigantic hound (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L711-L714`).
5. Mortimer consults Holmes. Sir Henry receives a warning pasted from *The Times*; a boot is stolen; a bearded man follows them in a cab, and gives the cabman Holmes's own name (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1190-L1328`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1392-L1429`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1528-L1539`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L2037-L2115`).
6. Watson goes to the Hall; the convict Selden, out of Princetown three days, is loose on the moor (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L2327-L2341`). Barrymore signals to Selden with a candle (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L3686-L3775`).
7. Holmes, secretly in a stone hut, is supplied by Cartwright (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L4897-L4928`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5042-L5083`). Selden, wearing Sir Henry's cast-off clothes, is hunted down by the hound and killed (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5363-L5376`).
8. Holmes recognises Stapleton in the portrait of Hugo (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5701-L5743`), and proves that Beryl is Stapleton's wife (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5130-L5142`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5914-L5947`).
9. At Merripit House, in fog, the hound attacks Sir Henry; Holmes kills it (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6195-L6241`). Beryl is found tied up (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6325-L6344`); Stapleton flees into the mire and is not seen again (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6433-L6441`).

## Apparent explanation

The family curse: a spectral hound (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L496-L505`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L656`).

## Culprit and motive

Jack Stapleton, for the Baskerville estate (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6551-L6557`).

## Evidence and investigative techniques

- Mortimer's stick: "friends of the C.C.H.", read as Charing Cross Hospital and confirmed in the Medical Directory; a dog's teeth marks (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L29`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L122`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L164-L171`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L184-L185`).
- The tiptoe prints in the yew alley: "He was running, Watson--running desperately" (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1112-L1121`).
- The warning is cut from *The Times*; Holmes knows its "leaded bourgeois type" (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1232-L1328`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1295`).
- The first, new boot is returned when it proves useless for scent; an old one is taken (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L1392-L1429`) (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6421-L6424`).
- The scent of white jessamine on the warning letter (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6724-L6728`).
- The portrait of Hugo (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L5701-L5743`).

## Red herrings and misdirection

The Barrymores, Selden, the bearded man, Frankland's telescope, the legend itself.

## Twists

The friendly naturalist is the heir; his "sister" is his wife; the bearded spy used Holmes's own name.

## Ethical questions

- Beryl, bruised and with a "red weal of a whiplash across her neck", was tied up by her husband, yet had helped him (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6325-L6356`); Laura Lyons was manipulated with a promise of marriage.
- Barrymore keeps faith with his wife's brother, a murderer (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L3816`).
- Holmes uses Sir Henry as bait, nearly fatally (`text:the-hound-of-the-baskervilles.txt#L6262-L6270`).

## Proposal (not canon): nuclear-cyberpunk adaptation

A legacy HTGR estate on wild, contaminated moorland, with a local legend of a "glowing beast" in the exclusion zone. A cousin who has been written out of the family fakes the legend with a bioluminescent or radioluminescent-painted animal, and frightens a heart-weak heir to death. The science hook is that radioluminescent paint is not the same as phosphorescence and leaves a measurable trace, so the "uncanny" glow can be tested with a detector. Proposal only; the physics would need the scientific director's review.
