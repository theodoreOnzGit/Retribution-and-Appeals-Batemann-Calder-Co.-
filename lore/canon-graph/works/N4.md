## Relationships

John Douglas is Birdy Edwards of Pinkerton's, who worked in Vermissa as Jack McMurdo (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6539-L6572`). His first wife, Ettie Shafter, died in California (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6637-L6638`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6666-L6669`). In California he made a fortune with Cecil Barker at Benito Canyon (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6669-L6671`), and later married his second wife in England (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6674`); she signs herself Ivy Douglas (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6727`). Barker was his best man (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1770-L1773`), and Douglas was jealous of the friendship between Barker and his wife (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1812-L1822`). In Vermissa, McMurdo lodges first with Jacob Shafter and then with Mike Scanlan at the Widow MacNamara's (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3401-L3402`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L4329-L4336`).

## Actual chronology

1. Part 2: "McMurdo" arrives in the Vermissa Valley, lodges with Jacob Shafter, falls in love with Ettie, and rises in Lodge 341 under Boss McGinty (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3218`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3575`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L4325`).
2. He is the Pinkerton man Birdy Edwards, who poses as a Chicago coiner on the run for the shooting of Jonas Pinto (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L4153-L4186`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6590-L6596`), and is branded at his initiation (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L4568-L4601`). When Brother Morris shows him a letter saying that Birdy Edwards is at work in the valley (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L5976-L5996`), he tells the lodge that Edwards is a "Steve Wilson" at Hobson's Patch and offers to bring him to his lodgings (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6227-L6297`); there he lures the leaders into a trap and McGinty and eight of his chief followers go to the scaffold and fifty-odd to prison (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6153-L6172`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6302`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6539-L6606`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6651-L6653`).
3. He marries Ettie in Chicago; the survivors, among them Ted Baldwin, hunt him for ten years, through Chicago and California, where Ettie dies (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6637-L6671`).
4. He settles at Birlstone Manor, raising the drawbridge every night (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1592`).
5. Porlock, inside Moriarty's organisation, sends Holmes a cipher warning of "deviltry" against Douglas at Birlstone, then withdraws (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L42-L109`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L307-L321`).
6. On the night of 6 January (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L875`) Baldwin, who had registered as "Hargrave" at the Eagle Commercial in Tunbridge Wells (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2443-L2449`), waits behind the study curtain; he attacks with a knife, Douglas strikes him with a hammer, and Baldwin is killed by his own sawed-off shotgun in the struggle for it: "Maybe it was I that pulled the trigger" (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3102-L3119`). The shot was about a quarter to eleven; the alarm was given half an hour later (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2257-L2283`).
7. Douglas and Barker dress the body in Douglas's ~~clothes, hide the brand,~~ **corrected 2026-10-17** dressing gown; they did not hide the brand: Baldwin bore the same lodge brand as Douglas, and seeing it gave Douglas the idea (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3132-L3147`). They put Douglas's rings and a piece of plaster on the body, sink Baldwin's clothes in the moat weighted with a dumb-bell, and Barker marks the window-sill with his bloodstained slipper (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3145-L3158`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3171-L3175`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2058-L2075`); Douglas hides in the house's old hiding place (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3134-L3172`). The wedding ring will not come off Douglas's finger, so the murderer appears to have taken it (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1078-L1089`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3149`).
8. Holmes, MacDonald and White Mason arrive; Holmes fixes on the missing dumb-bell, announces the moat will be drained, and catches Barker fishing out the bundle (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2197-L2206`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2771`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2883-L2888`).
9. Douglas comes out of hiding and hands over his manuscript, which becomes Part 2 (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2993-L3000`).
10. Epilogue: acquitted at the Quarter Sessions on grounds of self-defence, Douglas sails with his wife for South Africa and is lost overboard off St. Helena; Holmes sees Moriarty's hand (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6686-L6687`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6716-L6745`).

## Apparent explanation

Douglas murdered in his study by an outsider who waded the moat and escaped (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1422-L1427`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1503`).

## Culprit and motive

Ted Baldwin intended murder and died in the attempt; Douglas killed him in self-defence. The real culprit of the book is the lodge's vengeance, completed by Moriarty, who is hired for it (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L654-L660`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6743-L6745`).

## Evidence and investigative techniques

- The book cipher, page 534, two columns, solved with Whitaker's Almanac (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L215-L307`).
- MacDonald and Holmes on Moriarty and the Greuze painting, and on Jonathan Wild (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L497-L627`).
- The missing wedding ring below another ring (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1089`).
- The plaster on the dead man's jaw and the brand on his forearm (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1449-L1459`). Both were turned against the inquiry, and neither identifies the dead man: the plaster was put on the body to match the one Douglas was wearing, "You slipped up there, Mr. Holmes" (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3155-L3158`), and the brand is the lodge mark that Douglas also bears (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L3136-L3138`).
- The candle and the lamp, and Mrs. Allen's "slamming" door: the shot came half an hour before the alarm (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1850-L1877`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2257-L2275`).
- Barker's bloodstained slipper fits the mark on the sill (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2058-L2075`).
- The shotgun marked "PEN": the Pennsylvania Small Arms Company (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1168-L1182`).
- Holmes fishes the bundle out with Watson's umbrella and Ames's help, then puts it back (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2897-L2906`).
- One dumb-bell (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1529-L1536`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2205-L2206`).
- The Tunbridge Wells bicycle (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1549-L1554`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2443-L2465`).
- Draining the moat as a bluff (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2771`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2910-L2911`).
- The bundle: American boots, an overcoat from Neal, Outfitter, Vermissa (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2917-L2936`).

## Red herrings and misdirection

The bicycle "escape", the card "V.V. 341" (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1008`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L1480`), and Barker's footprint on the sill (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L2253`).

## Twists

The dead man is the murderer; the victim is hiding in his own house. And Part 2's McMurdo, apparently the worst of the Scowrers, is the detective (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6541-L6572`).

## Ethical questions

- Edwards spends years as an informant in a violent lodge, and even sets bombs to keep his cover while warning targets (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6602-L6606`).
- He is acquitted, yet the law cannot protect him from an organisation with reach.
- ~~MacDonald thinks Holmes's talk of Moriarty is close to libel (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L71`).~~ **corrected 2026-10-17** It is Holmes who tells Watson that calling Moriarty a criminal is "uttering libel in the eyes of the law" (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L69-L79`); MacDonald thinks Holmes has "a wee bit of a bee in your bonnet over this professor" (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L454-L458`).
- Holmes keeps faith with Porlock and never traces him (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L436-L444`).
- Edwards's cover means claiming murder and coining, and standing guard at the beating of the editor Stanger, which he stops short of killing (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L4914-L4946`) (`text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L6600-L6602`).

## Proposal (not canon): nuclear-cyberpunk adaptation

A corporate whistleblower once worked undercover inside a union-busting gang in a company mining town and brought its leaders to trial. Years later, in a fortified estate, an assassin dies in a struggle and the whistleblower lets the world think he is dead. The player solves the "locked" estate, then learns that a protected witness with a new identity is still vulnerable to an organisation with reach into institutions, which echoes the Meridian Compact without settling anything about it.
