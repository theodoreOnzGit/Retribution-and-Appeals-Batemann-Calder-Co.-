# Audit of the story drafts

> **Status: proposal, not canon.** The storyline agent's own audit of the
> drafts in `story/` (brief §16 item 9): plot holes, unfair clues, weak
> motivations and continuity risks, each with what was done about it.
> **Status** is one of: *Fixed* (changed in the drafts), *Mitigated*
> (reduced but still present), *Left open* (needs the owner's decision or
> the scientific director's review).

## 1. Plot holes

| # | Problem | Status | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Why would an expert inspector use a means that bioassay can reveal?** | Mitigated | Julian relied on three things he could see: the proxy power to refuse further tests (E12), HALCYON's employer-first configuration (E11), and the fast-track coroner (E24). He did not know about Barts' 30-day biobank, a recent hospital practice outside his field (SQ-27). He also acted in haste: four days between the vault notification (D−21) and the dinner (D−19), with the deadline of the 14th. `vale-and-the-compact.md` §7 makes "urgent crimes are committed by individuals, badly" a principle. A sharp player may still ask; Julian's confession can answer: "I thought nobody would ever ask the right question. Nobody did, until you." |
| P2 | **Why not simply deny 2158 when Elias went to the Directorate?** | Fixed | Elias told Julian at D−25 that he had kept copies of the original survey sheets for thirty-one years (added to the timeline), so denial was impossible. |
| P3 | **Why didn't Julian destroy Elias's copies after his death?** | Fixed | They are in Elias's encrypted personal vault. Julian offered on D+4 to "sort out Dad's papers" for Mara, and she had not got round to it (added to E29 as a small extra clue). |
| P4 | **The vault flag arrives exactly when Julian needs a cover. Coincidence?** | Fixed | Not a coincidence: the chain is causal. Elias's decision (D−25) led to the vault entry (D−23), which triggered the automated audit (D−21). Julian decided to act *because* the flag gave him a cover; the timeline places his "no action" mark (D−20) the day before the dinner. |
| P5 | **How did a principal inspector come to have regulated material?** | Left open | The drafts show only the record (a lot "verified returned" by him that the supplier never received, E19, E20) and plant his orphan-source work (opening §4). How he came to have it is deliberately never shown (safety boundary). Risk: some players may find a senior regulator handling material implausible. **Owner's decision:** accept as is, or add a scene establishing that inspectors personally supervise recovery of orphaned material from bankrupt yards. |
| P6 | **Could the cleaner have been harmed?** | Left open | The deep clean on D−18 means another person entered the flat. The drafts say only "faint traces, no hazard" weeks later. Whether Ada Morrow should be found, by bioassay, to have a small harmless intake (more evidence, more moral weight against Julian) is a choice for the owner and the scientific director (SQ-31). |
| P7 | **Why didn't Voss, the expert, suspect internal contamination while Elias was alive?** | Fixed | She did not know his symptoms: Barts would not admit a non-relative, and his illness was described to her only as "radiation, occupational". She learned the vault could not explain it only from the player, after the death. |
| P8 | **Why is Julian, not Mara, the medical proxy?** | Fixed | Designated in 2171 when Ruth was dying and Mara was eleven, never updated. Planted in the opening (Mara: "He signed all my school forms when Mum was ill"). |
| P9 | **A 2189 hospital that does not order bioassay seems negligent.** | Mitigated | The omission is split between protocol (employer assessment plus negative counter routes to an external-exposure pathway), configuration (HALCYON rates the test "low yield") and the proxy's refusal. Real precedent exists for delay (SQ-52). Still worth the scientific director's judgement. |
| P10 | **How does the player learn the biobank exists in time?** | Fixed | Two routes: Voss, or Dr Amari at Barts (added to E13). |
| P11 | **The intake-date precision may be unrealistic.** | Mitigated | If SQ-28 requires a wider window that includes the vault entry, E16 (provenance) excludes the vault on chemistry instead; `branches.md` §3 already uses this route when samples are lost. |
| P12 | **The death course versus HALCYON's 3 to 4 Gy estimate.** | Left open | A 3 to 4 Gy external dose with good 2189 care might not kill in three weeks; if so, HALCYON's own estimate is inconsistent with the death. That could be a fair extra clue, but it must be decided, not stumbled into (SQ-14). |

## 2. Unfair or too-fair clues

| # | Problem | Status | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | **The "Jules cooks" deduction depends on a remark in the opening chapter.** | Mitigated | Said twice (opening §4 and §7) and written to the player's journal automatically (E26). |
| U2 | **The proxy reveal (E12) could point at Julian too early.** | Fixed | Gated until after E13; and written so that the compassionate reading is fully available. |
| U3 | **Julian's line "One of these days I'll find out the stuff never went anywhere at all" (opening §4) may be too on-the-nose.** | Left open | It is fair (planted before the crime) and reads as ordinary grumbling on first play, but a sharp player may remember it at E19 and leap. **Owner's choice:** keep it, or cut the second sentence and leave only the paperwork complaint. |
| U4 | **Clues depending on a single dialogue choice.** | Fixed | Every gated clue has at least two routes (evidence-matrix "Where / how"). |
| U5 | **Science needed before it is taught.** | Fixed | Each Insight needed for a deduction is unlocked earlier: dose rate × time and "what a dosimeter records" on the dock; what a counter can see and chain of custody at Voss's lab, all in the opening chapter. |
| U6 | **The *Silver Blaze* deduction (E17) narrows but does not prove.** | Intended | It is presented as narrowing suspicion, and the surveys (E18) do the proving. The evidence matrix says so. |
| U7 | **The contaminant is unnamed, so players cannot reason from its properties.** | Mitigated | The case never asks the player to identify it; it asks what instruments can and cannot see, which is taught generically. The director may still decide to name it (SQ-21). |
| U8 | **Physics alone could seem to "prove murder".** | Fixed | The evidence matrix states that Questions 1 to 3 establish only a non-occupational internal intake, consistent with accident and suicide; who and why rest on records and testimony (brief §4, §9). |

## 3. Weak motivations

| # | Problem | Status | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Is career, liberty and Compact fear enough for Julian to kill his oldest friend?** | Mitigated, left partly open | Layered motive: prosecution for falsifying a statutory record; the end of a 31-year career on the eve of becoming Chief Inspector; fear of the people behind 2158; a self-justifying story that exposure would wreck the Belvedere redevelopment. Still the emotional weak point. **Suggestion:** an optional scene, before the death, in which Julian says "Some of us have more to lose than our names", and a confession line that lets him be pitiable without being excused. |
| M2 | **Why did Elias warn Julian first?** | Fixed | Loyalty, and the hope that they could confess together; set up by their friendship and his remark "They'd need a friend. I have one of those. That's the difficulty" (opening §7). |
| M3 | **Why does Voss keep silent for days?** | Mitigated | Licence risk, Mara's dislike, private grief. Softened by the bequest letter and the holiday booking she can show. She remains a little unsympathetic, which serves the red herring. |
| M4 | **Why does Crane keep the car-park promise after Elias's death?** | Mitigated | He promised, and he distrusts investigators until the player shows real evidence. Could be strengthened by having him fear B&C would use the conversation against the Reach. |
| M5 | **Why did Elias sign in 2158?** | Fixed | Just married; told he would never work again. (An earlier draft said Ruth was pregnant with Mara, which contradicted Mara's age; corrected.) |
| M6 | **Hale's refusal of the sample request looks like plot convenience.** | Mitigated | It follows a standing rule (legacy packages are not opened outside a scheduled campaign) and makes Hale a better red herring. |

## 5. Safety review

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Any method of obtaining, preparing, delivering or concealing radioactive material described? | No. The hidden truth says only that an intake occurred during a given evening; the act is never shown. |
| Any quantities of radioactive material? | No. The only numbers are doses and dose rates as investigators read them from records and instruments, all marked as placeholders. |
| Any real radionuclide presented as a poison? | No. The contaminant is unnamed (SQ-21). |
| Detection-evasion guidance? | No. Instrument limitations are discussed only as the investigator's lesson ("a negative answers only the question the instrument asks"), which the brief requires (§4), and the culprit's plan rests on institutional habits, not on technical evasion. |
| Does evidence automatically prove murder? | No (see U8). |
| Is nuclear technology portrayed as inherently evil? | No. world-bible §2.4 sets the rule; in the case, measurement solves the crime and a falsified record and a betrayal cause it. |
