## Use of real cases

Real inquiries and judgments are cited for **how institutions and people
behaved and how the truth came out**: what was falsified, who was trusted,
who was dismissed, how findings were reopened. The Litvinenko Inquiry is
cited only for its investigative reasoning and institutional findings; this
file records nothing about how any material was obtained, prepared,
delivered or concealed.

## Literature checks on behaviour

1. **LB-1. The motive is the drafts' weakest-sourced link** (BC-01; audit
   M1). Research supports each ingredient: escalation of commitment when one
   is personally responsible for a bad outcome `[src: staw1976,
   sleesman2012]`; self-justification by "moral justification" that harm
   serves a worthy end `[src: bandura1999]`; and in homicidal poisoning the
   offender is usually personally involved with the victim and often a
   caregiver `[src: steckflynn2007]`, which fits Julian as friend and medical
   proxy. **No empirical study was found** on how often people kill a friend
   to prevent exposure of an old wrongdoing; that step is unsourced. The audit
   already flags the motive as the emotional weak point; the literature
   agrees, and suggests strengthening the escalation (each act of concealment
   makes the next one necessary) rather than adding new motives.
2. **LB-2. Honest institutions trusting the wrong record is very well
   documented** (BC-05 to BC-07). The Post Office prosecutions rested on a
   legal presumption that computers work correctly `[src: marshall2021]`,
   later shown unjustified `[src: bates2019]`; automation bias persists in
   experts and is not removed by training `[src: parasuraman2010]`. This is
   the case's strongest-grounded idea.
3. **LB-3. Appeals that reopen old findings take decades and are driven by
   families** (BC-13, BC-14). Hillsborough's verdicts were quashed 23 years
   later on fresh evidence `[src: hillsborough-quash2012, hip2012]`;
   Post Office convictions were quashed on Criminal Cases Review Commission
   references 8–18 years after conviction (convictions 2003–2013, quashed 2021) `[src: hamilton2021]`. The
   drafts' proposal that a finding procured by a false record can be reopened
   at any time on fresh evidence (open decision 24) matches how the real s.13
   route has been used; whether 2189 imposes a time limit stays open.
4. **LB-4. Mara's compensation dilemma has a real shape, not a real rule.**
   No-fault radiation compensation exists `[src: csrld]`, but no source was
   found for a scheme whose award is suspended by an appeal. Keep it marked
   as an invention (BC-15).
