## How these sources were found, and what that means for trust

- **Every entry was located with a web search in the session of 2026-10-17.**
  None was added from memory. Where a search could not confirm a source or a
  detail (a DOI, a page number, a specific figure), that is said in the entry,
  and the claim tables do not rely on the unconfirmed part.
- **No page was read in full.** The page-fetching tool failed for every host
  in this session (DNS lookup errors), so what each entry "establishes" was
  read from the search results: abstracts, publisher records, official
  summaries and quoted passages. Where the only confirmation came from a
  secondary source (a news report, a law firm's summary, a training slide
  deck), the entry says **(secondary)**. A reviewer should treat every entry
  as "found and summarised", not "read and checked line by line".
- **Accessed** is 2026-10-17 for every entry.
- **Fiction is not evidence.** Doyle's canon is cited elsewhere as
  `text:<file>#L<line>` and never appears here.
- **Safety.** Entries on real poisonings and radiological incidents record
  only investigative reasoning, clinical course and institutional findings.
  None records how any material was obtained, prepared, delivered or
  concealed, and none gives quantities of material.

## 1. Dose quantities, units and limits

### `icrp103`
- **Reference:** ICRP (2007). *The 2007 Recommendations of the International Commission on Radiological Protection.* ICRP Publication 103. Annals of the ICRP 37(2–4).
- **ID:** URL https://www.icrp.org/docs/icrp_publication_103-annals_of_the_icrp_37(2-4)-free_extract.pdf (DOI not confirmed by search; not given)
- **Establishes:** Replaces the 1990 Recommendations; updates radiation and tissue weighting factors; keeps the occupational and public dose limits unchanged. Radiation weighting factors 1 for photons, 20 for alpha particles, and a continuous function of energy for neutrons (Eq. 4.3), confirmed through the Canadian Radiation Protection Regulations, which refer to that curve, and the European Nuclear Society glossary (both secondary).

### `icrp147`
- **Reference:** ICRP (2021). *Use of Dose Quantities in Radiological Protection.* ICRP Publication 147. Annals of the ICRP 50(1).
- **ID:** DOI 10.1177/0146645320911864
- **Establishes:** Effective dose is a risk-adjusted quantity for managing protection against stochastic effects (principally cancer) and for comparison with limits, constraints and reference levels; it is at most an approximate indicator of risk, and best estimates of risk to an individual use organ doses and specific risk models.

### `icrpaedia-cd`
- **Reference:** ICRP. "Committed effective dose", ICRPaedia glossary entry (May 2019 glossary).
- **ID:** URL https://icrpaedia.org/Committed_effective_dose
- **Establishes:** Committed effective dose is the weighted sum of committed organ equivalent doses after an intake; the commitment period is 50 years for adults and to age 70 for children. Corroborated by the BfS (German Federal Office for Radiation Protection) glossary.

### `icrpaedia-hp`
- **Reference:** ICRP. "Personal dose equivalent", ICRPaedia glossary entry.
- **ID:** URL https://icrpaedia.org/Personal_dose_equivalent
- **Establishes:** Personal dose equivalent Hp(d) is the dose equivalent in soft tissue at depth d below a specified point on the body, usually where the dosimeter is worn; Hp(10) for penetrating radiation and effective-dose monitoring, Hp(0.07) for skin, hands and feet. (ICRU Reports 39/51 are the original definitions, not read; ICRU Report 95 redefines the quantity.)

### `bipm-cipm1984`
- **Reference:** BIPM, CIPM Recommendation 1 (1984), concerning the sievert; revised by CIPM Recommendation 2 (2002).
- **ID:** URL https://www.bipm.org/en/-/committees/ci/cipm/73-1984/resolution-1
- **Establishes:** The gray is the special name for the joule per kilogram when used for absorbed dose, and the sievert for dose equivalent; dose equivalent is absorbed dose multiplied by ICRP-specified factors, so a number in J/kg can mean different things for the two quantities, and the special names exist to prevent that confusion.

### `iaea-gsr3`
- **Reference:** IAEA (2014). *Radiation Protection and Safety of Radiation Sources: International Basic Safety Standards.* IAEA Safety Standards Series No. GSR Part 3.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-92-0-135310-8; URL https://www.iaea.org/node/40219
- **Establishes:** Occupational effective-dose limit of 20 mSv per year averaged over five consecutive years (100 mSv in 5 years), with no more than 50 mSv in any single year; lens-of-the-eye limit on the same pattern; 500 mSv per year to extremities and skin (limit values confirmed through secondary summaries quoting the text).

### `iaea-gsg2`
- **Reference:** IAEA (2011). *Criteria for Use in Preparedness and Response for a Nuclear or Radiological Emergency.* General Safety Guide GSG-2 (partly superseded by GSR Part 7, 2015).
- **ID:** URL https://iaea.org/sites/default/files/dpp527.pdf (IAEA document proposal describing GSG-2's supersession) (secondary)
- **Establishes:** For severe deterministic effects, emergency criteria are expressed as RBE-weighted absorbed dose to organs or tissues (Gy-Eq), not effective dose; stochastic risk uses equivalent and effective dose.

## 2. Clinical effects of radiation

### `waselenko2004`
- **Reference:** Waselenko JK, MacVittie TJ, Blakely WF, et al. (2004). Medical management of the acute radiation syndrome: recommendations of the Strategic National Stockpile Radiation Working Group. *Annals of Internal Medicine* 140(12):1037–1051.
- **ID:** URL https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA523724.pdf (full text copy)
- **Establishes:** Individual dose after an exposure is estimated from time to onset and severity of nausea and vomiting, the decline in absolute lymphocyte count over hours to days, and the appearance of chromosome aberrations; treatment of the haematopoietic syndrome is dose-based (cytokines, transfusion, selected stem-cell transplantation). The paper's LD50 figures were **not** confirmed by search.

### `rpor-ars`
- **Reference:** "Medical management of the acute radiation syndrome", review article in *Reports of Practical Oncology and Radiotherapy* (Via Medica). Authors and year not confirmed by search.
- **ID:** URL https://journals.viamedica.pl/rpor/article/view/73290
- **Establishes:** Phases of acute radiation syndrome: prodromal (0–2 days from exposure), latent (2–20 days), manifest illness (21–60 days). The CDC's physician fact sheet (`cdc-ars`) describes the latent phase as dose-dependent, from hours to weeks.

### `cdc-ars`
- **Reference:** US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. *Acute Radiation Syndrome: A Fact Sheet for Clinicians.*
- **ID:** URL https://cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/media/pdfs/ARS.pdf
- **Establishes:** Prodromal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, anorexia, possibly diarrhoea) occur from minutes to days after exposure; the latent stage lasts from hours to weeks; the manifest stage from hours to months, all depending on dose.

### `dainiak2011`
- **Reference:** Dainiak N, et al. (2011). Literature review and global consensus on management of acute radiation syndrome affecting nonhematopoietic organ systems. *Disaster Medicine and Public Health Preparedness* 5(3):183–201.
- **ID:** DOI 10.1001/dmp.2011.73; PMC3638239
- **Establishes:** A WHO expert panel found no high-quality human trials of countermeasures for non-therapeutic radiation exposure; recommendations rest on limited human and animal data.

### `icrp118`
- **Reference:** ICRP (2012). *ICRP Statement on Tissue Reactions and Early and Late Effects of Radiation in Normal Tissues and Organs: Threshold Doses for Tissue Reactions in a Radiation Protection Context.* ICRP Publication 118. Annals of the ICRP 41(1–2).
- **ID:** URL https://icrpaedia.org/ICRP_Publication_118 (DOI not confirmed by search; not given)
- **Establishes:** Updated "practical" thresholds for tissue reactions defined at 1 % incidence; 0.5 Gy for lens and circulatory disease regardless of dose rate. A secondary summary (Hong Kong Observatory) attributes to the ICRP a bone-marrow threshold of about 0.5 Gy acute or over 0.4 Gy/year protracted. Skin thresholds were **not** confirmed.

### `iaea-biodos2011`
- **Reference:** IAEA (2011). *Cytogenetic Dosimetry: Applications in Preparedness for and Response to Radiation Emergencies.* EPR-Biodosimetry 2011.
- **ID:** URL https://www-pub.iaea.org/mtcd/publications/pdf/epr-biodosimetry%202011_web.pdf
- **Establishes:** Standard methods for biological dose assessment after accidental exposure; the dicentric chromosome assay is the "gold standard", calibrated with a linear-quadratic dose–effect curve; translocation, PCC and micronucleus assays as supplements (scope confirmed; chapter text not read).

### `ainsbury2023`
- **Reference:** Ainsbury E (2023). Biological dosimetry lecture, EURADOS Winter School 2023 (slides).
- **ID:** URL https://eurados.sckcen.be/sites/eurados/files/uploads/Actions/Winter%20schools/AM2023/6_Ainsbury.pdf
- **Establishes:** Dicentric yields reflect an averaged whole-body dose; inhomogeneous exposure, high-LET radiation and internally deposited radionuclides complicate cytogenetic dose estimates. (Slides; secondary.) A UK HPA report (RPD-012) adds that background and counting statistics stop the assay showing a true zero, so many low-dose results come back as "zero" estimates.

### `rcpath2015`
- **Reference:** Royal College of Pathologists and Institute of Biomedical Science (2015). *The Retention and Storage of Pathological Records and Specimens*, 5th ed. (superseded by a 6th edition in force from October 2025).
- **ID:** URL https://rcpath.org/resourceLibrary/the-retention-and-storage-of-pathological-records-and-specimens--5th-edition-.html
- **Establishes:** UK laboratories work to published minimum retention periods for specimens and records (e.g. 30 years for tissue blocks in the 6th edition). The retention period for routine blood and urine samples was **not** confirmed by search.

### `nathwani2016`
- **Reference:** Nathwani AC, Down JF, Goldstone J, et al. (2016). Polonium-210 poisoning: a first-hand account. *The Lancet* 388(10049):1075–1080.
- **ID:** DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(16)00144-6; open manuscript https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10046853
- **Establishes (clinical course and diagnostic reasoning only):** the patient was first treated for presumed gastroenteritis; low platelets and neutrophils within about six days were first attributed to a drug effect; hair loss and mucositis by two weeks; a chemical poison was investigated and set aside; multi-organ failure within about three weeks and cardiac arrest on day 23. A standard Geiger counter scan over the body did not detect the emission; an atypical course led to considering radioactive poisoning, and the diagnosis was confirmed by gamma-ray spectroscopy of a urine sample. Early symptoms overlap with many toxins, so diagnosis can be missed without a high index of suspicion.

## 3. External monitoring and calibration

### `iaea-orp-internal`
- **Reference:** IAEA, Occupational Radiation Protection Networks (ORPNET): webinar "Criteria and practice of internal dosimetry", and training material based on GSG-7.
- **ID:** URL https://iaea.org/resources/webinar/criteria-and-practice-of-internal-dosimetry
- **Establishes:** Doses from intakes are assessed indirectly, from activity in the body or organs, in excreta, or in air, interpreted with ICRP biokinetic and dosimetric models; GSR Part 3 and GSG-7 set separate monitoring requirements for internal exposure. (Secondary: an IAEA-affiliated article adds that dosimetry badges measure external exposure and cannot determine what has entered the body.)

### `iaea-unit6`
- **Reference:** IAEA, training module "Unit 6: Indirect methods, individual and workplace monitoring" (GSG-7 training series).
- **ID:** URL https://iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/04/unit6_indirectmethods_indiv_workplace_monitoring.pdf
- **Establishes:** Excreta monitoring may be the only technique for radionuclides with no or only low-energy photon emissions; alpha spectrometry of excreta requires radiochemical separation before measurement.

### `iaea-unit9`
- **Reference:** IAEA, training module "Unit 9: Assessment of intake and dose" (GSG-7 training series).
- **ID:** URL https://iaea.org/sites/default/files/22/04/unit9_assessment_intakeanddose.pdf
- **Establishes:** The time of intake (elapsed time between intake and measurement) is among the information an assessor must know or assume; early urinary excretion is variable.

### `kry2016-tg191`
- **Reference:** Kry SF, et al. AAPM Task Group 191 presentation on clinical use of luminescent dosimeters (2016), Imaging and Radiation Oncology Core, MD Anderson.
- **ID:** URL https://irochouston.mdanderson.org/RPC/Publications/RPC_Presentations/2016_files/Kry%20AAPM%20TG191%202016.pdf
- **Establishes:** Luminescent dosimeter signal fades over time (a few per cent in days, then about 1 % per month for the cited TLD case), which is characterised and corrected; readings are corrected for background, linearity, beam quality and angle. (Slide deck; secondary.)

### `nrc-transit`
- **Reference:** US NRC, presentation on dosimeter background and transit dose (ADAMS ML24008A038).
- **ID:** URL https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2400/ML24008A038.pdf
- **Establishes:** Passive dosimeters pick up transit dose in shipping, and control badges are used to subtract it; one shipment's control value may not fit another. (Slide deck; secondary.)

### `icrp75-via-apac2020`
- **Reference:** "Uncertainty evaluation in measurement of the personal dose equivalent at nine individual monitoring services in Asia and the Pacific region", *Radiation Protection Dosimetry* (2020). Authors not confirmed by search.
- **ID:** URL https://oap.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/S26-การประเมินความไม่แน่นอนในการวัดปริมาณส่วนบุคคลทั้งเก้าแห่งในเอเชียและภูมิภาคแป_compressed.pdf (hosted copy)
- **Establishes:** Expanded uncertainties of Hp(10) at nine monitoring services were 24–86 % (average 38 %) near 1 mSv and 14–40 % (average 27 %) near the 20 mSv annual limit, all inside the ICRP's accepted accuracy of **a factor of 1.5 in either direction** for doses near the limits (ICRP Publication 75, cited by the paper; ICRP 75 itself not read).

### `eurados-ic`
- **Reference:** EURADOS Working Group 2, intercomparisons for whole-body dosemeters (IC2008 onward); EURADOS Report 2023-01.
- **ID:** URL https://eurados.sckcen.be/en/news-overview/eurados-intercomparison-2024-whole-body-dosemeters-ic2024ph
- **Establishes:** Dosimetry services take part in blind intercomparisons judged against a "trumpet curve" criterion (ISO 14146 / IEC 62387); in IC2010 86 % of systems met it. Intercomparison is the standard way calibration drift and systematic error in a dosimetry service are exposed.

### `nist-gmp13`
- **Reference:** NIST Office of Weights and Measures. *GMP 13: Good Measurement Practice for Ensuring Metrological Traceability* (2019), citing JCGM 200:2012 (VIM).
- **ID:** URL https://www.nist.gov/document/gmp-13-ensuring-traceability-20190621pdf
- **Establishes:** Metrological traceability is the property of a measurement result that can be related to a reference through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the uncertainty; traceability belongs to results, not instruments, and does not by itself guarantee adequate uncertainty or the absence of mistakes. (VIM wording confirmed through secondary reproductions.)

### `iso17025`
- **Reference:** ISO/IEC 17025:2017. *General requirements for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories.* Clause 7.7.
- **ID:** URL https://sadcas.org/sites/default/files/pdf-documents/SADCAS%20F%2061%20%28e%29%20-%20Proficiency%20Testing%20Requirements%20ISO-IEC%2017025-2017%20Clause%207.7%20%26%20SADCAS%20Requirements%20[Issue%203].pdf (accreditation body's requirements document quoting the clause) (secondary)
- **Establishes:** Accredited laboratories must monitor the validity of results through proficiency testing or interlaboratory comparison, and must act on monitoring results outside pre-defined criteria to prevent incorrect results being reported.

### `orau-portal`
- **Reference:** Oak Ridge Associated Universities, Radiation Emergency Assistance Center/Training Site. *Portal Monitor Job Aid.*
- **ID:** URL https://www.orau.gov/rsb/vcrc/application/Resources/JobAids/PortalMonitorJobAid.pdf
- **Establishes:** Portal monitors screen people for **external** contamination and detect gamma and beta radiation but not alpha. (Dedicated alpha-capable personnel monitors exist; none of these instruments measures internal deposition.)

### `canteach-isl`
- **Reference:** CANTEACH (Canadian nuclear training library), radiation protection chapter, document 20051604.
- **ID:** URL https://ww.nuceng.ca/canteach-rev2/library/20051604.pdf
- **Establishes:** The inverse-square law applies to gamma radiation from point sources (largest dimension under about a fifth of the distance), not to beams; dose accumulates as dose rate multiplied by stay time.

### `cember2009`
- **Reference:** Cember H, Johnson TE (2009). *Introduction to Health Physics*, 4th ed. McGraw-Hill Medical.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-0-07-142308-3
- **Establishes:** Standard health-physics textbook; recorded as the textbook reference for dose rate, time, distance and shielding. **Contents not checked in this session** (only the edition was confirmed), so no claim rests on it alone.

## 4. Internal monitoring and bioassay

### `icrp130`
- **Reference:** Paquet F, Etherington G, Bailey MR, Leggett RW, et al. (ICRP 2015). *Occupational Intakes of Radionuclides: Part 1.* ICRP Publication 130. Annals of the ICRP 44(2):5–188.
- **ID:** URL https://icrpaedia.org/ICRP_Publication_130 (PubMed 26494836; full DOI not confirmed)
- **Establishes:** Revised biokinetic models for uptake, retention and excretion by element and organ; data for interpreting bioassay. Interpretation depends on assumptions about the intake pattern, the physical and chemical form of the material, and the time elapsed before measurement.

### `iaea-srs37`
- **Reference:** IAEA (2004). *Methods for Assessing Occupational Radiation Doses due to Intakes of Radionuclides.* Safety Reports Series No. 37.
- **ID:** ISBN 92-0-103904-2; URL https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/P1190_web.pdf
- **Establishes:** Practical guidance on interpreting monitoring data and assessing committed effective dose from intakes (contents beyond the scope statement not read).

### `nrc-wbc`
- **Reference:** US NRC, training presentation on internal dosimetry and whole-body counting (ADAMS ML111711590).
- **ID:** URL https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1117/ML111711590.pdf
- **Establishes:** A whole-body count responds only to gamma radiation; nuclides that decay by alpha and beta emission cannot be detected by it and must be measured in vitro. (Slide deck; secondary.)

### `currie1968`
- **Reference:** Currie LA (1968). Limits for qualitative detection and quantitative determination: application to radiochemistry. *Analytical Chemistry* 40(3):586–593.
- **ID:** DOI 10.1021/ac60259a007
- **Establishes:** Hypothesis-testing definitions of the critical level, detection limit and determination limit for counting measurements; showed that eight common recipes for "detection limit" differed by a factor of nearly 1000 for the same case.

### `iso28218`
- **Reference:** ISO 28218:2010. *Radiation protection — Performance criteria for radiobioassay* (confirmed 2021).
- **ID:** URL https://www.iso.org/standard/40869.html
- **Establishes:** Performance criteria for in vivo and in vitro bioassay laboratories: accuracy, minimum detection-limit requirements, test levels, reporting and QA/QC; uses the ISO 11929 detection-limit concept.

### `astm-c1473`
- **Reference:** ASTM C1473-00. *Standard Test Method for Radiochemical Determination of Uranium Isotopes in Urine by Alpha Spectrometry.*
- **ID:** URL https://softbank.iust.ac.ir/Railway/Codes/ASTM-standard/ASTM_2004_Standards/ASTM%202004%20PART%20III/ASTM%202004%20Volume%2012.01%20Nuclear%20Energy%20(I)/C%201473%20-%2000%20%20_QZE0NZM_.pdf (hosted copy)
- **Establishes:** Urine radiobioassay adds an isotopic tracer before chemical separation so that chemical yield and counting efficiency can be determined for each sample.

### `ideas2006`
- **Reference:** Doerfel H, et al. (2006). *General Guidelines for the Estimation of Committed Effective Dose from Incorporation Monitoring Data* (IDEAS project). Report FZKA 7243, Forschungszentrum Karlsruhe; also EURADOS Report 2013-01.
- **ID:** URL https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/270065490/3814835
- **Establishes:** Harmonised procedure for assessing intakes and doses from monitoring data. Software built on the method (GuiAR, secondary) determines an unknown intake date by maximum-likelihood fitting of the measurement series. The precision achievable was **not** confirmed.

### `ncrp161`
- **Reference:** NCRP (2008). *Management of Persons Contaminated with Radionuclides.* NCRP Report No. 161 (Handbook and Scientific and Technical Bases).
- **ID:** URL https://ncrponline.org/wp-content/themes/ncrp/PDFs/ExecSumm_NCRP-Report-No-161.pdf
- **Establishes:** Guidance for responders to radionuclide contamination; the aim of treatment is to reduce the risk of health effects early after the incident or later in life. (The timing of decorporation therapy was **not** confirmed from the search.)

## 5. Forensics, custody and provenance

### `mayer2005`
- **Reference:** Mayer K, Wallenius M, Ray I (2005). Nuclear forensics — a methodology providing clues on the origin of illicitly trafficked nuclear materials. *Analyst* 130:433–441.
- **ID:** DOI 10.1039/B412922A
- **Establishes:** Nuclear forensics reconstructs the history of material of unknown origin from signatures such as isotopic composition, chemical impurities and microstructure, which carry the imprint of the processes the material went through.

### `iaea-nss2g`
- **Reference:** IAEA (2015). *Nuclear Forensics in Support of Investigations.* IAEA Nuclear Security Series No. 2-G (Rev. 1), Implementing Guide.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-92-0-102115-1; URL https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1687web-74206224.pdf
- **Establishes:** Guidance on the role of nuclear forensics in criminal investigations involving radioactive material, including staged laboratory reporting (24 hours, one week, two months after sample receipt).

### `stanley2012`
- **Reference:** Stanley FE (2012). A beginner's guide to uranium chronometry in nuclear forensics and safeguards. *Journal of Analytical Atomic Spectrometry* 27(11).
- **ID:** URL https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2012/ja/c2ja30182b
- **Establishes:** Radiochronometry estimates the time since a material's last chemical purification from parent–daughter ratios (Bateman equations), assuming no daughter at purification and a closed system since. Related IAEA material lists other chronometer pairs and reports interlaboratory validation against materials of known production date.

### `nist-coc`
- **Reference:** NIST Computer Security Resource Center glossary, "chain of custody", citing NIST SP 800-72.
- **ID:** URL https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/chain_of_custody
- **Establishes:** Chain of custody is a process that tracks evidence from collection through analysis by documenting each person who handled it, the date and time of each collection or transfer, and the purpose of each transfer.

### `litvinenko2016`
- **Reference:** Sir Robert Owen (Chairman) (2016). *The Litvinenko Inquiry: Report into the death of Alexander Litvinenko.* HC 695. London: House of Commons, 21 January 2016.
- **ID:** URL https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8055c340f0b62302692e48/The-Litvinenko-Inquiry-H-C-695-web.pdf
- **Establishes (investigative reasoning and findings only):** The inquiry relied on a trail of radioactive contamination found at many sites the men had visited (offices, hotels, a stadium, aircraft) to reconstruct movements and timing, and on that trail found an earlier attempt two weeks before the fatal one (Part 8, Chapter 4). It reached its conclusion as a "strong circumstantial case" built from forensic, documentary and witness evidence together. Its institutional findings attributed responsibility to two named men acting probably at the direction of a state security service. The Metropolitan Police told the inquiry that the cause would not have been identified by normal post-mortem toxicology.

### `chemworld2016`
- **Reference:** Chemistry World (2016). "Anonymous forensic investigators and chemists tracked trail left by dissident's killers" (news report on evidence given to the Litvinenko Inquiry).
- **ID:** URL https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/litvinenko-inquiry-unveils-scientific-sleuths-at-heart-of-investigation/9385.article
- **Establishes (investigative reasoning only):** A first indication in a urine sample was confirmed by a second, independent laboratory technique before the finding was accepted; scientists compared contamination levels along the trail to reason about where the primary contamination occurred; a scientist testified that trace contamination could not identify the source reactor, which corrected early press claims. (Secondary.)

## 6. Molten-salt reactors (LFTR)

### `haubenreich1970`
- **Reference:** Haubenreich PN, Engel JR (1970). Experience with the Molten-Salt Reactor Experiment. *Nuclear Applications and Technology* 8(2):118–136.
- **ID:** DOI 10.13182/NT8-2-118
- **Establishes:** The MSRE was an 8 MW(th) reactor with molten fluoride fuel salt circulating through graphite; it ran on U-235 from June 1965 (9000 equivalent full-power hours by March 1968); the uranium was removed by on-site fluorination and U-233 added, making the MSRE the first reactor fuelled with U-233; U-233 operation began in October 1968.

### `msbr-recycle1970`
- **Reference:** ORNL authors (1970). MSBR fuel recycle (processing plant for the Molten Salt Breeder Reactor). *Nuclear Applications and Technology*; ORNL technical memoranda on protactinium isolation (ORNL-TM-3137, -3138, -3579).
- **ID:** URL https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/openmsr/msr-archive/master/docs/NAT_MSBRrecycle.pdf
- **Establishes:** The MSBR concept used a 7LiF–BeF2–ThF4 fuel salt with about 0.3 mole % 233UF4; its processing plant was to isolate Pa-233 from the neutron flux and remove rare-earth fission products, on processing cycles of days.

### `serp2014`
- **Reference:** Serp J, Allibert M, Beneš O, et al. (2014). The molten salt reactor (MSR) in generation IV: overview and perspectives. *Progress in Nuclear Energy* 77:308–319.
- **ID:** URL https://lpsc.in2p3.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/ArticlePNE2014_MSR-GIF_final.pdf (DOI not confirmed)
- **Establishes:** Overview of Generation IV MSR research: the MSRE (1965–69) is the main operating experience; current programmes (fast-spectrum MSRs in the EU, France and Russia; FHR in the USA; solid-fuel precursors in China) are research and development, i.e. molten-salt fuel processing is not demonstrated at commercial scale.

### `ornl-tm3464`
- **Reference:** ORNL-TM-3464 (MSRE noble-gas behaviour) and ORNL-4069 (MSRE xenon poisoning); ORNL fission-product volatility report Pub129748.
- **ID:** URL https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/openmsr/msr-archive/master/docs/ORNL-TM-3464.pdf
- **Establishes:** Noble-gas fission products have very low solubility in molten salt and are removed continuously by the off-gas system; MSRE xenon poisoning was about 1.4 % without circulating bubbles. MSR off-gas systems separate fission products from the fuel as a matter of routine operation.

### `mo99-msr2024`
- **Reference:** "Evaluation of 99Mo production based on molten salt reactor off-gas extraction", *He Jishu* (Nuclear Techniques), August 2024.
- **ID:** URL https://doaj.org/article/bc5159e2cce94d15a9151b3bc995666c
- **Establishes:** A modelling study (on MSRE data) estimating medical-isotope production from MSR off-gas; earlier studies had only estimated production in the core. Shows that extracting radioisotopes from MSR fuel salt or off-gas is an active research proposal, not current practice.

### `dostal2004`
- **Reference:** Dostal V, Driscoll MJ, Hejzlar P (2004). *A Supercritical Carbon Dioxide Cycle for Next Generation Nuclear Reactors.* MIT-ANP-PR-100, MIT Center for Advanced Nuclear Energy Systems.
- **ID:** URL https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/67671

## 12. Behavioural and organisational research

- **Establishes:** 1921 cases from 138 countries; the longer a perpetrator has worked for an organisation, the costlier the fraud tends to be; owners and executives were involved in 19 % of cases but caused the highest median losses.

### `stigler1971`
- **Reference:** Stigler GJ (1971). The theory of economic regulation. *Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science* 2(1):3–21.
- **ID:** URL https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/bios/Stigler.html (biographical entry quoting the article)
- **Establishes:** "Regulation is acquired by the industry and is designed and operated primarily for its benefit": regulated firms capture the regulator and use regulation to restrict competition (the founding statement of regulatory capture; contested and refined since, e.g. by Peltzman).

### `voicingloss2024`
- **Reference:** Jacobson J, et al. (2024). *Voicing Loss: bereaved people's experiences of coroners' investigations and inquests.* Institute for Crime & Justice Policy Research (Birkbeck) and University of Bath.
- **ID:** URL https://icpr.org.uk/node/383
- **Establishes:** Interviews with 89 bereaved people: many found inquests alienating and disempowering; families brought three hopes (the truth about the death, lessons to prevent future deaths, justice and accountability), and there was a considerable gap between what they expected and what they experienced.

### `shalowitz2006`
- **Reference:** Shalowitz DI, Garrett-Mayer E, Wendler D (2006). The accuracy of surrogate decision makers: a systematic review. *Archives of Internal Medicine* 166(5):493–497.
- **ID:** URL https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16534034/
- **Establishes:** Across 16 studies (2595 surrogate–patient pairs), surrogates predicted patients' treatment preferences with 68 % accuracy; neither patient designation of the surrogate nor prior discussion improved accuracy.

### `taylormoss2013`
- **Reference:** Taylor CR, Moss DL (2013). *The Fertilizer Oligopoly: The Case for Global Antitrust Enforcement.* American Antitrust Institute Working Paper 13-05.
- **ID:** URL https://www.antitrustinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/FertilizerMonograph.pdf
- **Establishes:** Members of government-sanctioned fertiliser export associations (PhosChem, Canpotex) exercised market power over 1998–2012, as estimated by dynamic Lerner indices and anecdotal evidence; the authors call for coordinated competition enforcement. (Commentators note oligopoly pricing is hard to prove.)

### `cochabamba2003`
- **Reference:** "The Water War in Cochabamba, Bolivia: Privatization Triggers an Uprising." *Mountain Research and Development* 23(1):98 (2003), "A Personal View". Author not confirmed by search.
- **ID:** DOI 10.1659/0276-4741(2003)023[0098:APV]2.0.CO;2
- **Establishes:** A water concession to a Bechtel-led consortium (Aguas del Tunari) raised rates sharply; a mass uprising in 2000, including irrigators' associations, forced the government to end the contract, and a later law treated water as a public good. Secondary sources add that rates rose by up to 100–200 % and that the company's claim against Bolivia was settled in 2006.

## 13. Documented cases: inquiries, judgments and investigations

### `bates2019`
- **Reference:** *Bates and Others v Post Office Ltd (No 6) "Horizon Issues"* [2019] EWHC 3408 (QB), Fraser J, 16 December 2019.
- **ID:** URL https://www.benthamsgaze.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Bates-and-Ors-v-the-Post-Office-Ltd-2019-EWHC-3408-QB.pdf
- **Establishes:** Bugs, errors and defects in the Horizon accounting system could and did cause apparent shortfalls in branch accounts; the system was not remotely robust in its early form; Post Office and Fujitsu could access and edit transaction data remotely. The confidence the Post Office routinely expressed in the system was not justified.

### `hamilton2021`
- **Reference:** *Hamilton and Others v Post Office Ltd* [2021] EWCA Crim 577, Court of Appeal, 23 April 2021.
- **ID:** URL https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Hamilton-Others-v-Post-Office-summary-230421.pdf
- **Establishes:** On references from the Criminal Cases Review Commission, the Court of Appeal quashed 39 convictions of sub-postmasters for dishonesty: the Post Office's failures of investigation and disclosure about Horizon's reliability made the prosecutions an affront to the conscience of the court.

### `marshall2021`
- **Reference:** Marshall P, Christie J, Ladkin PB, Littlewood B, Mason S, Newby M, Rogers J, Thimbleby H, Thomas M (2021). Recommendations for the probity of computer evidence. *Digital Evidence and Electronic Signature Law Review* 18:18–26.
- **ID:** URL https://journals.sas.ac.uk/deeslr/article/view/5240
- **Establishes:** Courts in England and Wales presume that computers were working correctly unless there is evidence to the contrary (a presumption restored after s.69 PACE 1984 was repealed in 1999); the authors argue the presumption is unwarranted for software and propose a two-stage disclosure process; the Post Office relied on it in prosecuting sub-postmasters.

### `hip2012`
- **Reference:** Hillsborough Independent Panel (2012). *Hillsborough: The Report of the Hillsborough Independent Panel.* HC 581. London: The Stationery Office, 12 September 2012.
- **ID:** URL https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-report-of-the-hillsborough-independent-panel
- **Establishes:** 164 police statements were significantly amended, 116 of them to remove or alter criticism of the policing; police ran checks on the dead to impugn their reputations; the families had been right that the authorities sought to create an unjust account blaming the fans; the panel reviewed over 450 000 pages of documents.

### `hillsborough-quash2012`
- **Reference:** *HM Attorney General v HM Coroner of South Yorkshire (West)*, High Court, 19 December 2012 (application under s.13 Coroners Act 1988).
- **ID:** URL https://gov.uk/government/news/attorney-general-to-apply-for-fresh-inquests-for-victims-of-hillsborough-disaster-16-october-2012
- **Establishes:** The 1991 accidental-death verdicts were quashed 23 years after the disaster on fresh evidence brought to light by the panel; fresh evidence that might shed light on the cause of death normally makes a new inquest necessary or desirable in the interests of justice; the new inquests (2014–2016) ran for over 300 days.

### `jones2017`
- **Reference:** Jones J (2017). *"The Patronising Disposition of Unaccountable Power": A report to ensure the pain and suffering of the Hillsborough families is not repeated.* HC 511, 1 November 2017.
- **ID:** URL https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a821d79ed915d74e6235dce/6_3860_HO_Hillsborough_Report_2017_FINAL_WEB_updated.pdf
- **Establishes:** Names the pattern bereaved families met whenever they challenged public and private authorities; 25 points of learning including a charter for bereaved families, their proper participation at inquests and an independent public advocate.

### `grenfell2024`
- **Reference:** Grenfell Tower Inquiry (2024). *Phase 2 Report*, HC 19, 4 September 2024 (Chairman Sir Martin Moore-Bick).
- **ID:** URL https://grenfelltowerinquiry.org.uk/sites/default/files/CCS0923434692-004_GTI%20Phase%202%20Volume%201_LARGE_3%20SEPT_0.pdf
- **Establishes:** Product manufacturers engaged in "systematic dishonesty": deliberate and sustained strategies to manipulate testing, misrepresent test data and mislead the market; the strategies succeeded largely because the certifier was incompetent and accommodated its customers; the 72 deaths were wholly avoidable.

### `hannaattisha2016`
- **Reference:** Hanna-Attisha M, LaChance J, Sadler RC, Schnepp AC (2016). Elevated blood lead levels in children associated with the Flint drinking water crisis: a spatial analysis of risk and public health response. *American Journal of Public Health* 106(2):283–290.
- **ID:** DOI 10.2105/AJPH.2015.303003
- **Establishes:** An independent paediatric study found the incidence of elevated blood lead in children under 5 rose from 2.4 % to 4.9 % after the water source change, with the largest rises in disadvantaged neighbourhoods and no change outside the city.

### `fwatf2016`
- **Reference:** Flint Water Advisory Task Force (2016). *Final Report*, 21 March 2016. Office of the Governor of Michigan.
- **ID:** URL https://www.michigan.gov/documents/snyder/FWATF_FINAL_REPORT_21March2016_517805_7.pdf
- **Establishes:** The state environmental agency bears primary responsibility; its response to outside experts was "aggressive dismissal, belittlement, and attempts to discredit these efforts and the individuals involved"; the Governor's office kept relying on incorrect agency information despite mounting outside evidence; the crisis was a case of environmental injustice.

### `naiic2012`
- **Reference:** National Diet of Japan, Fukushima Nuclear Accident Independent Investigation Commission (2012). *The Official Report* (Chairman Kurokawa K).
- **ID:** URL https://www.neimagazine.com/analysis/a-manmade-accident/ (secondary analysis); URL https://kiyoshikurokawa.com/en/?p=1349 (chairman's note on submission)
- **Establishes:** The accident was "clearly manmade"; the relationship between the regulator (NISA) and the operator amounted to regulatory capture, in which the regulator effectively let the operator regulate itself and apply rules voluntarily. (Report confirmed through secondary coverage; official PDF not reached.)

### `tepco2002`
- **Reference:** Reuters Events, "Tokyo Electric Power has a history of mismanagement and serious safety lapses"; Citizens' Nuclear Information Center, *Nuke Info Tokyo* No. 92 and 118.
- **ID:** URL https://www.reutersevents.com/sustainability/node/35656 ; URL https://nirs.org/wp-content/uploads/reactorwatch/accidents/nit92.pdf
- **Establishes:** A former GE inspection engineer reported in 2000 that TEPCO had falsified inspection records and hidden cracks; it became public in 2002; TEPCO admitted faking safety reports for more than a decade and shut all 17 of its reactors temporarily; four directors resigned. Reuters reports that the regulator disclosed the whistleblower's identity to TEPCO and that he was dismissed and blacklisted (reported, not independently confirmed).

### `clark1997`
- **Reference:** Clark C (1997). *Radium Girls: Women and Industrial Health Reform, 1910–1935.* University of North Carolina Press.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-0-8078-4640-7
- **Establishes:** The radium industry resisted classifying radium as a poison; company and state medical investigators had economic and political ties that kept them from defending workers' health; scientific recognition of radium poisoning came only in 1925, under pressure from the Consumers' League (from reviews).

### `shipman2003`
- **Reference:** Smith J (2003). *The Shipman Inquiry, Third Report: Death Certification and the Investigation of Deaths by Coroners.* Cm 5854.
- **ID:** URL https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-shipman-inquiry-third-report-death-certification-and-the-investigation-of-deaths-by-coroners
- **Establishes:** Death certification, cremation certification and coroners' investigations failed both to deter and to detect a doctor who killed at least 215 patients, because the system relied on the word of the trusted certifying doctor; recommended an independent death investigation system able to detect homicide.

## 14. Law and procedure (England and Wales)

### `coroners1988-s13`
- **Reference:** Coroners Act 1988, s.13 (order to hold a fresh inquest), as applied in later case law (e.g. *HM Senior Coroner for Cornwall* [2024] EWHC 2673 (Admin)).
- **ID:** URL https://www.5essex.co.uk/correcting-the-record-s-13-coroners-act-1988-applications-for-a-fresh-inquest/ (barristers' commentary quoting the section) (secondary)
- **Establishes:** With the Attorney General's authority (fiat), the High Court may quash an inquest and order a fresh one where it is necessary or desirable in the interests of justice, including for fraud, irregularity, insufficient inquiry or new facts or evidence.

### `coroners-r23`
- **Reference:** Coroners (Inquests) Rules 2013, r.23 (written evidence); Coroners and Justice Act 2009, s.9C (inquests in writing, in force 28 June 2022); Chief Coroner's Guidance No. 29.
- **ID:** URL https://www.judiciary.uk/guidance-and-resources/chief-coroners-guidance-no-29-inquests-in-writing-and-rule-23-evidence/
- **Establishes:** A coroner may admit written evidence where it is unlikely to be disputed (a "documentary inquest"), and since 2022 may conclude an inquest in writing without a hearing if no one reasonably asks for one and there is no real prospect of disagreement. These are the present-day analogue of the drafts' "fast-track on the papers".

### `mca2005`
- **Reference:** Mental Capacity Act 2005 (c. 9), s.4 (best interests), with s.11 and Schedule 1 (lasting powers of attorney).
- **ID:** URL https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/enacted/data.html
- **Establishes:** Anyone deciding for a person who lacks capacity, including a health-and-welfare attorney, must act in the person's best interests, weighing their past and present wishes, beliefs and values, and consulting those engaged in their care; s.4(5): a decision-maker on life-sustaining treatment must not be motivated by a desire to bring about the person's death. (The provision giving an attorney power to refuse treatment was not confirmed by search.)
