# The Quiet Dose: hidden truth (three options)

> **Status: proposal, not canon.** The brief (§9, §15) leaves the culprit and
> the complete chronology undecided. This file offers three complete hidden
> chronologies, recommends one, and gives reasons. The other files in this
> folder (`evidence-matrix.md`, `red-herrings.md`, `branches.md`) are written
> for the recommended option. Doyle material is cited as
> `text:<file>#L<line>`; everything else is invented.

## Safety boundary for every option

This case is loosely inspired by real radiological forensic investigations
(brief §9 names the Litvinenko case). The drafts follow one rule:

**The story describes only what investigators measure and reason about:**
records, timestamps, instrument readings, bioassay results, calibration,
chain of custody, testimony and documents. **It never describes how a
radioactive material was obtained, prepared, delivered or concealed**, gives
no quantities, and names no real radionuclide as a poison. In the options
below, "an intake occurred during a given window" is the most the hidden
truth says about the act itself. The contaminant is referred to as
**"the contaminant"**; its identity is for the scientific director to
choose, or to leave unnamed (SQ-21).

## What is fixed in all three options

These come from the brief and are kept in every option:

- Elias Ward, a long-serving nuclear engineer, dies under suspicious
  circumstances.
- The official finding is death from **cumulative occupational radiation
  exposure**, supported by a **corporate medical AI**.
- His daughter **Mara Ward** suspects foul play.
- An **independent forensic investigation** finds inconsistencies between the
  official exposure history and the medical evidence.
- **Dr Evelyn Voss**, a scientist or physician, has a complicated relationship
  with him.
- **Anthony Vale** is a possible benefactor with hidden connections.
- The science undermines the official story **without by itself proving
  murder**.

## Option A (recommended): "The Friend Who Did Not Report"

**Culprit:** Julian Marsh.
**Manner:** deliberate killing, disguised by the institutions' own habits.

### A.1 The secret behind the case (2158)

On 14 March 2158, a fuel-handling fault at Erith Reach (then an operating
HTGR) released contamination through a ventilation path into the station's
east yard and onto the new workers' housing at Belvedere Flats. The off-site
survey was carried out by two 30-year-old engineers, **Elias Ward and Julian
Marsh**, under the shift chief. Their survey found deposition above the level
that should have triggered relocation and long-term health monitoring.

The station's management had the survey rewritten so that every value fell
below the action level. The clean-up waste (soil, filters, yard material) was
removed quietly as "routine maintenance waste" and placed in **Vault 3**,
declared with inventories that matched the rewritten survey. Ward and Marsh
were told to countersign the rewritten survey. Both did. Ward did it because
he and Ruth had just married and he was told he would never work again if he
refused; Marsh did it because he wanted the career that followed.

Who leaned on them is open. It can be a Compact act, or the act of people who
later joined the Compact (see `story/vale-and-the-compact.md` §4).

**What is and is not true about the harm.** The deposition was real and above
the action level. Whether it made anyone in Belvedere Flats ill is genuinely
uncertain: the expected effects of such exposures are statistical increases in
long-term risk, not individual cases anyone could point to. The story should
not claim more than that (SQ-39). What is certain is that the families were
denied the relocation, monitoring and compensation the law required.

### A.2 Why it surfaced in 2188

In 2186 Erith Reach entered decommissioning and Elias moved into waste
characterisation. In autumn 2188 his routine non-destructive scans of the
Vault 3 packages gave activities far above their declared inventories. Each
time, the discrepancy was recorded in B&C's characterisation system and
"reconciled" by a standard correction for legacy measurement uncertainty.
Elias recognised the packages. He knew what was in them.

He decided to confess, to reopen the Belvedere Flats case, and to take the
consequences. Before he did, he wanted independent measurements that no one
inside B&C's systems could "reconcile" away.

### A.3 Motive

Julian Marsh stands to lose everything if the 2158 survey is exposed: his
nomination as Chief Inspector (confirmation hearing set for 30 days after
Elias's death), his career, his liberty (falsifying a statutory record), and
the protection of the people who arranged the 2158 settlement, some of whom
are now in the Compact and would hold him responsible. He tells himself a
second story too: that exposure now would wreck the Erith Reach
decommissioning settlement and the Belvedere Flats redevelopment (part-funded
by the Vale Foundation), harming the very families Elias wants to help. Vale
gave no order. Julian acted on what he believed the system needed and on what
he needed. He sits in the "shared responsibility, high culpability" cell of
the Compact member map.

### A.5 What physically happened (investigator's level)

1. **The Vault 3 entry gave Elias a small external dose**, of the order the
   vault's own survey maps predict for 22 minutes: far below anything that
   causes illness. Voss's passive dosimeter recorded it. His electronic
   dosimeter, docked, recorded nothing, as it should have.
2. **On D−19, Elias took the contaminant into his body.** From then on, it
   irradiated him from inside, concentrated in the organs its chemistry
   favours (to be set by the scientific director, SQ-21, SQ-22). The organ
   doses were high enough to cause bone-marrow failure and multi-organ injury
   over about three weeks (SQ-12, SQ-13).
3. **No dosimeter could have recorded this.** A personal dosimeter measures
   radiation arriving at the dosimeter from outside the body. It records
   nothing about material inside the body (SQ-15).
4. **The whole-body count was correctly performed and correctly negative**,
   because the instrument was not built to detect the radiation the
   contaminant gives off in measurable amounts outside the body (SQ-18,
   SQ-19). It answered a different question from the one that mattered.
5. **His urine and blood, kept in the Barts biobank, carried the evidence the
   whole time** (SQ-23 to SQ-27).
   Modelling the series of samples places the intake between **D−21 and
   D−17, most likely D−19** (SQ-28). Three events fall in that window: the
   supper at Voss's lab (D−20), the dinner at Julian's (D−19) and the union
   meeting (D−17).
6. **The contaminant has a chemical and isotopic fingerprint** that matches a
   class of radiocatalysis feedstock and **does not match** the Erith Reach
   legacy material (SQ-29, SQ-30). It was not occupational.
7. **The Directorate's source register shows one lot of that class**, from a
   radiocatalysis yard in Dagenham that went bankrupt in 2187, as "returned to
   supplier; verified J. Marsh". The supplier has no record of receiving it.
   How Julian came to have it, and what he did with it, are never shown; the
   register discrepancy is all the story needs.
8. **Faint residual traces with the same fingerprint** remain in Julian's
   dining room and kitchen despite the deep clean, at levels of no hazard to
   anyone, detectable only by a forensic survey under court order (SQ-31).
   Elias's bathroom at home carries traces from after D−19, consistent with
   an internal intake on or before that date. No matching traces are found at
   Voss's lab, the union hall, Elias's car or his office.

### A.6 How the official story was built (nobody needed to lie except Julian)

This is the case's main idea: **the official story was built by honest
institutions reasoning from the wrong instrument.**

- Hale filed a true notification and a true note.
- HALCYON reasoned **backwards from a target**: it estimated a dose from the
  blood counts and then looked for an occupational event big enough to supply
  it, found the Vault 3 note, and attributed the dose to it without checking
  whether the vault could deliver it. When the biodosimetry disagreed, it
  discarded the biodosimetry.
- Barts trusted the employer's exposure assessment, as protocol says.
- The whole-body counter told the truth about what it can see.
- The coroner followed the fast-track rule.
- Julian's only acts after the intake were a "no action" mark, a proxy
  decision that looked compassionate, a phone call that looked kind, and help
  with the compensation forms that looked kinder still.

Julian's plan rested on exactly this. Like Oberstein putting the papers in
Cadogan West's pocket so that "the whole business will assuredly be put to
his account" (text:his-last-bow-03-the-bruce-partington-plans.txt#L1275-L1277),
he let the Vault 3 note put Elias's death to Elias's own account.

### A.8 Why Option A is recommended

1. **The science is honest and teaches the brief's core distinctions.** The
   radiation injury is real; what is wrong is the pathway (external versus
   internal), the instrument (dosimeter and counter versus bioassay) and the
   reasoning (backwards from a target). Every scientific subject listed in
   brief §9 has a natural place.
2. **The evidence undermines the official story without proving murder**
   (brief §9). The physical evidence establishes a non-occupational internal
   intake of material from outside Erith Reach. On its own it fits accident
   and suicide as well as murder. Only records, testimony and character
   establish who and why, and intent stays a matter for a court.
3. **Human motivation leads** (brief §12.5): a thirty-year friendship, a
   shared guilt, one man's decision to confess and the other's decision not
   to let him.
4. **The twist reinterprets earlier information** (§12.4). Julian's
   strictness, his cooking, his kindness to Mara, his help with the forms and
   his concern at the bedside are all shown before or early in the case and
   all mean something else at the end.
5. **It begins the long arc without exposing Vale** (brief §8). Julian is a
   Compact member acting without orders, exactly as §7 allows. Vale's own
   charity (Voss's lab) makes the truth measurable.
6. **It uses the "Appeals" theme mechanically.** The payout depends on
   accepting the finding; the appeal suspends it. Mara must choose.
7. **It keeps nuclear technology on the right side** (world-bible §2.4).
   Measurement solves the case; a falsified record and a betrayal cause it.

## Option B: "The Second Shift"

**Culprit:** no murderer. A negligent employer and a cover-up.
**Manner:** accidental external over-exposure in an unlicensed workplace,
hidden by folding it into the occupational record.

### B.1 Summary

Elias, proud and private, had been secretly working night shifts at an
unlicensed radiocatalysis cell in Dagenham run by **Ostler Process
Services** (invented), a small supplier in B&C's supply chain. He needed the
money: Mara's housing contract was moving her to the civic tier, and he
refused to let her lose priority power for the medical device she depends on
(a detail the opening chapter would establish). The yard's owner, **Ned
Ostler**, had bypassed a shielding interlock to speed throughput. On D−20 the
bypass let Elias stand in an engineered radiation field for several minutes.

When Elias fell ill, Ostler's backers inside B&C, who rely on such cells for
cheap throughput, had Hale's legitimate Vault 3 note (Elias really did enter
the vault, for reasons connected to his own work) inflated by HALCYON into an
explanation. The record was not falsified; the interpretation was steered.

### B.2 Timeline (abridged)

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| D−90 | Elias starts night shifts at Ostler's cell, paid off the books |
| D−23 | Vault 3 entry (as in Option A, but for a routine reason) |
| D−20, 02:15 | Interlock bypass; Elias exposed. Ostler's interlock log shows no alarms for six weeks, because the alarm was bypassed with the interlock. |
| D−19 | Ostler wipes the cell's camera storage |
| D−12 to D0 | Admission, HALCYON attribution to Vault 3, decline, death |
| D+3 | Occupational finding |

### B.3 What physically happened

A real, partly non-uniform external exposure: biodosimetry shows a
substantial dose (unlike Option A), skin changes on one side of his body
point to the direction of the field (SQ-42), and bioassay is negative. The
dose cannot have come from Vault 3 (dose rate × time), so it came from
somewhere else. Transit logs, a pay chit in his coat (the *Silver Blaze* bill
in the pocket, text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L1048-L1060) and
Ostler's silent alarm log (the dog that did not bark,
text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L846-L848) lead to the cell.

### B.4 Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strengths:** the safest science (no internal contamination at all);
  the strongest inequality theme; a faithful *Silver Blaze* structure ("the
  real murderer is standing immediately behind you", the horse,
  text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L956-L961).
- **Weaknesses:** no murderer may feel anticlimactic for a first case; Mara's
  suspicion of foul play is only partly vindicated; the *Dying Detective* and
  the Compact link are thinner; external-only evidence teaches less about
  internal contamination, which the brief singles out.

## Option C: "The Expert"

**Culprit:** Dr Evelyn Voss.
**Manner:** deliberate killing by the expert in exactly this kind of death;
she then leads the "independent" investigation to steer it away from herself.

### C.1 Summary

For six years Voss's laboratory, kept alive by a Vale Foundation grant and a
B&C bioassay contract, has issued some routine worker bioassay results
without running the full analysis, to keep up with volume and keep the
contract. Most were harmless; two concealed real intakes in Rotherhithe gig
workers. Elias, as her partner, found the discrepancy in her lab records on
D−30 and told her she had two weeks to report herself. On D−19 Elias's intake
occurs at her flat (never shown). She then lets HALCYON's occupational story
stand until Mara starts asking, at which point she "discovers" the
inconsistencies herself and points the player at B&C and Hale.

### C.2 Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strengths:** the most faithful *Dying Detective* structure (the expert
  in the disease is its author, text:his-last-bow-04-the-dying-detective.txt#L287-L297);
  a big twist; the investigator-culprit forces the player to replicate
  results independently.
- **Weaknesses:** it makes the game's first scientific mentor a liar, which
  undercuts the "measurement is the hero" principle and makes the player
  distrust science teaching for the rest of the game; it makes Vale's real
  charity the instrument of a crime, which pulls against "his kindness must
  not all be revealed as fake" (brief §6); the brief describes Voss's
  relationship with the victim as "complicated", not culpable; and the
  physician-poisoner is a well-worn trope.

## Recommendation, in one paragraph

Choose **Option A**. It keeps the radiation injury real and moves the
mystery to the pathway, the instrument and the reasoning, which are the
distinctions the brief wants taught. It makes the evidence undermine the
official story without proving murder, gives the case a human core in a
betrayed friendship and a shared old guilt, lets Vale's genuine charity make
the truth measurable, and opens the Compact arc through a member acting
without orders. Option B's moonlighting and inequality material can be kept
as a side case in the same district; Option C's lab shortcuts can become a
smaller secret of Voss's that is never murderous (see `red-herrings.md`).
