# The Quiet Dose: red herrings (Option A)

> **Status: proposal, not canon.** Every red herring here is a person or
> theory with **real evidence for it** and a **real secret of its own**,
> which is why it misleads. Each is cleared by evidence the player can find,
> never by the game's say-so. Clue IDs refer to `evidence-matrix.md`.

## Principles

1. **Each suspect is guilty of something.** The canon's best red herrings are
   people with genuine secrets: Fitzroy Simpson really was touting, and his
   cravat really was in the dead man's hand
   (text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L274-L287); Silas Brown really
   did hide the horse (#L732-L746); Sidney Johnson is "unpopular with his
   colleagues" and alone has a safe key
   (text:his-last-bow-03-the-bruce-partington-plans.txt#L318-L324).
2. **Pursuing a red herring changes the world** (see `branches.md`), and
   sometimes does good: exposing Hale's calibration secret helps three hundred
   workers.
3. **No red herring is cleared by an alibi alone.** Each is cleared by a
   measurement or a document that the player has to reason about.

## R1. Dr Evelyn Voss: the expert (theory T4)

*Canon model:* Culverton Smith, "the man upon earth who is best versed in
this disease", who turns out to be its author
(text:his-last-bow-04-the-dying-detective.txt#L287-L297). Here the
resemblance is the trap.

**Why she looks guilty**
- She is the leading independent expert in internal dosimetry in the city.
- Her lab handles radioactive calibration standards.
- She lent Elias the equipment for an unauthorised vault entry (E07, E08).
- Elias had supper with her on D−20, inside the intake window (E17).
- She knew something was wrong on D−8 and told nobody.
- She finds the contaminant within a day of receiving the samples
  ("How did she know to look?").
- She inherits Elias's research notes and a sum of money; Mara did not know
  she was in the will.
- Mara resents her and says so.

**Her real secrets**
- She aided an unauthorised entry to a controlled area and could lose her
  licence for it.
- She was Elias's partner for four years and is grieving privately; she
  stayed away from the hospital and the funeral because Mara did not know.
- She knew from the bequest letter, received D+2, that Elias trusted her more
  than he trusted the institutions.

**What clears her**
- E14: an independent laboratory replicates her result. She did not invent
  the contaminant.
- E16: her calibration standards are of a different class from the
  contaminant, and her standards register balances to the last item.
- E18: no contaminant traces at her lab (only legacy-signature traces on the
  spectrometer case, from Vault 3).
- E17: the D−20 supper came from a shop and both ate it.
- Why she looked so quickly: she was told the vault could not explain the
  illness (E05) and knew what that left. She says so if asked, in a line that
  doubles as a lesson: "If it wasn't outside him, it was inside him. There is
  nowhere else for a dose to come from."

**When:** suspicion peaks around D+10 to D+14; cleared by about D+16.

## R2. Gideon Hale: the record-keeper (theory T3)

*Canon model:* Silas Brown, "a more perfect compound of the bully, coward,
and sneak" (text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L726-L728), who is
guilty of the lesser crime.

**Why he looks guilty**
- He controls every dose record at Erith Reach.
- He refused Elias's sample request on D−24 (E08's backstory).
- He flagged Elias's vault entry, which became the basis of the official
  story (E04, E21).
- He signed off the "reconciled" Vault 3 discrepancies (SQ-44).
- He is obstructive, defensive and rude.
- He is hiding something (E28).

**His real secret**
- E28: the site's dosimeter reader under-read by about 12 % for 18 months.
  He corrected it going forward and quietly did not back-correct the records,
  because it would have pushed eleven workers over their annual dose
  constraint on paper and cost B&C a contract penalty.
- *Literature check (2026-10-17):* a 12 % bias is inside the accuracy the
  ICRP accepts near the limits `[src: icrp75-via-apac2020]`; see E28's note
  for the proposed fix.

**What clears him**
- E05 + E07: the vault entry he flagged did no harm; nothing in his records
  could have hidden a factor of a thousand.
- E28 itself: 12 % cannot turn a few millisieverts into several gray.
- E21: he filed the notification honestly, on time, to the right desk.
- E16: the contaminant is not Erith Reach material.

**When:** suspicion peaks around D+7 to D+12.

## R3. Batemann & Calder Co.: the corporation (theory T2)

**Why it looks guilty**
- It runs Erith Reach (provisional, world-bible §8.4) and stands to pay for
  whatever the Vault 3 discrepancy reveals.
- HALCYON's configuration favours the employer's records (E11).
- Its lawyer, **Celia Strand**, offers Mara a "supplementary goodwill
  payment" if she does not appeal.
- A B&C security contractor follows Mara for three days from D+6.

**Its real secret**
- B&C does know the Vault 3 inventories are wrong (E08) and has been
  "reconciling" them to avoid reopening the 2158 history and its liability.
  That is a cover-up, not a killing.
- The man following Mara is there to find out whether she has her father's
  measurements.

**What clears it (of the killing)**
- E11: the HALCYON setting is a years-old default applied to every worker.
- E16: the contaminant is not from B&C's site.
- E19 + E20: the material's trail leads to a Directorate disposal record.

**When:** suspicion is constant in the background; it is the theory the Reach
families believe, and it is half right about the cover-up.

## R4. Tobias Crane: the angry neighbour (theory T5)

*Canon model:* Fitzroy Simpson: the stranger with a motive, a weapon-shaped
object and a public quarrel, whose own story was true
(text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L274-L293).

**Why he looks guilty**
- He accused Elias in public of "signing away the Reach" on D−17, inside the
  intake window, in front of forty witnesses (E17).
- Since his lay-off he has worked shifts at Dagenham radiocatalysis yards,
  including the very yard whose lot is missing (E19).
- He has a conviction for assault from a picket-line fight in 2182.

**His real secret**
- After the meeting, Elias went after him into the car park and told him:
  "Give me two weeks, Tobias, and the Reach will have what it needs." Crane
  has been collecting the Reach families' dose and health complaints for
  years. He knew Elias was about to help them and had every reason to keep
  him alive. He has not said so because he promised Elias to keep quiet.

**What clears him**
- E18: no traces at the union hall.
- E17: the meeting's food was a shared buffet.
- E15: the most likely intake date is D−19, not D−17.
- E19 + E20: the missing lot was "verified returned" by the Directorate,
  after Crane had left that yard.
- His own testimony, once he trusts the player.

**When:** suspicion peaks around D+12 to D+16, when E16 points at
radiocatalysis feedstock.

## R5. Suicide (theory T6)

**Why it looks plausible**
- Elias was secretive for weeks (the vault, Voss, the statement).
- The D−26 statement reads like a confession (E25).
- His symptoms began close to the anniversary of Ruth's death.
- B&C's lawyers adopt the theory as soon as T1 fails, because it also voids
  the compensation claim against the operator's levy.

**What answers it**
- E25: the statement was addressed to the Directorate with an appointment
  booked for the 14th.
- A holiday booked with Voss for the week after the 14th (Voss has the
  confirmation).
- E19 + E20: Elias had no access to the Dagenham lot.
- Character: the man who would not walk into a vault without a dosimeter
  (E26(e)).

## R6. Anthony Vale (theory T7)

**Why the player may suspect him**
- He met Elias at the Belvedere clinic opening in the opening chapter and
  asked whether he had been at Erith Reach "in '58".
- He funds Voss's laboratory.
- The Belvedere redevelopment he part-funds depends on the decommissioning
  settlement that Elias's confession would disrupt.
- (If the owner chooses world-bible §8.3 V1) he is publicly tied to B&C.

**The truth in Option A**
- He did not know of the killing beforehand. Whether he knew of the 2158
  falsification is left open by design.
- His question at the clinic was what it seemed: he had read Elias's 2160s
  papers on graphite characterisation and was curious.

**What answers it**
- No clue in this case connects Vale to the killing; the trail (E19, E20)
  leads to Julian.
- If accused, Vale answers calmly with a fact that is true and with a
  question the player cannot easily answer: "If I wanted a man silenced,
  would I pay for the laboratory that can measure what was done to him?"
- **Hook for later:** after the case, the player can learn that Vale
  arranged Julian's defence counsel, or declined to (see
  `story/vale-and-the-compact.md` §8).

## R7. Accident (theory T8)

**Why it looks plausible**
- Once the contaminant is found, Julian himself suggests it: "Belvedere's
  had legacy contamination for thirty years. You know that better than
  anyone now."
- That is a clever suggestion because it is partly true.

**What answers it**
- E16: the contaminant is radiocatalysis feedstock, not Erith Reach legacy
  material, so it is not Belvedere's old deposition.
- E18: none of Elias's ordinary places carry it before D−19.

## R8. Instrument red herrings

These mislead about *how*, not *who*:

- **The negative whole-body count** (E10) looks like proof there was no
  internal contamination. It was a correct measurement that could not see
  what mattered.
- **The "probable artefact" biodosimetry** (E09) looks like a lab error. It
  was the one test that disagreed with the official story, and it was right.
- **Hale's 12 % calibration error** (E28) looks like proof the records were
  doctored. It is real and irrelevant to the death.
- **HALCYON's priority setting** (E11) looks like a conspiracy. It is a
  default; the conspiracy, where there is one, is elsewhere.
