# Anthony Vale and the Meridian Compact: expansion proposals

> **Status: proposal, not canon.** Brief §6 (Vale) and §7 (the Compact) are
> **CANON: LOCKED**; this file adds to them and contradicts nothing in them.
> Brief §15 forbids silently settling Vale's age and genealogy, his absolute
> moral boundary, and the Compact's founding history: those appear here only
> as **options**, and none is chosen. Doyle material is cited as
> `text:<file>#L<line>`.

## 1. What is locked (restated so the additions can be checked against it)

- Vale is the principal long-form anti-villain: a celebrated philanthropist,
  scientific visionary and architect of modern nuclear and AI infrastructure.
- His charity is **real** and does real good: hospitals, education and
  scholarships, research, housing and worker assistance, public
  infrastructure.
- He secretly directs the Meridian Compact.
- He is a maternal-line descendant of Professor James Moriarty; the different
  surname hides it; the genealogy is a slow-burn revelation, **not proof of
  guilt**.
- He believes preventing systemic collapse can justify terrible sacrifices.
  Brilliant, persuasive, sometimes compassionate, sometimes ruthless; never
  cartoonish; sometimes **right on the facts** while wrong in his
  conclusions.
- He has **one firm moral boundary** (undecided), to be tested later at the
  cost of his empire.
- His kindness is not all fake. The player may love him and still hold him
  to account.
- The Compact is protected by fear, ideology, dependency, shared guilt, and
  loyalty or self-preservation. Members are unequally culpable. Some think it
  prevents collapse, some exploit it, some are trapped. **Vale does not
  necessarily order every crime**; members act on their own to protect the
  system, themselves, or what they think Vale wants. It is embedded in
  society, **not omnipotent**.

## 2. Vale: additions

### 2.1 A working portrait (proposal)

- **Public manner.** Quiet, exact, interested in other people's work. He
  remembers names and the details of a person's research. He funds things
  that do not make headlines: biobank freezers, dosimetry for gig-hall
  workers, scholarships for Reach families' children.
- **Private conviction.** Civilisation is a set of fragile machines that
  keep billions alive, and most people do not know how close to failure they
  run. Infrastructure must therefore be protected from politics, panic and
  revenge, including justified revenge. He has seen (or believes he has
  seen) what happens when a grid fails for a week.
- **Where he is factually right.** He is right that sudden exposure of old
  wrongs can destabilise essential services; right that public trust in
  nuclear power is fragile and that its loss would cost lives; right that
  some reformers would cause more harm than the abuses they oppose.
- **Where his conclusions go wrong.** He treats people's right to know the
  truth about their own bodies, homes and dead as something to be rationed
  for their own good; he treats complicity as a manageable cost; he
  underestimates how much the system's corruption is itself a risk of
  collapse.
- **What he admires.** Competence, honesty about evidence, people who change
  their minds when the data says so. This is why he can respect the player
  more after a principled disagreement (brief §11).

### 2.2 Model in the canon

Doyle's Moriarty is "a genius, a philosopher, an abstract thinker", who "does
little himself. He only plans", and whose agents, when caught, are given
"money … for his bail or his defence" while "the central power which uses
the agent is never caught--never so much as suspected"
(text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L9224-L9235). He is also publicly
spotless: "in calling Moriarty a criminal you are uttering libel in the eyes
of the law" (text:the-valley-of-fear.txt#L70-L71).

**What Vale keeps from that:** the respectability, the distance from the
act, the protection of agents. **What Vale changes:** Moriarty is
"the organizer of half that is evil" in London
(text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L9224-L9225) and has no good works;
Vale's good works are real, and the Compact is not a criminal enterprise
whose purpose is crime. It is a network whose purpose is stability, which
commits crimes when stability is threatened.

### 2.3 Age and genealogy

**Not decided (§15).** Constraint the drafts create: if the owner wants Vale
personally involved in the 2158 Erith Reach cover-up (one option in the
first case's backstory), he must have been an adult in 2158, i.e. born
before about 2138, so at least about 51 in 2189. If not, the cover-up can be
the act of people who later joined the Compact, and Vale can be any age.

## 3. Candidate moral boundaries (none chosen)

Each is something Vale will not do *even to save the system*. The brief
wants a future conflict that tests it at the cost of his empire.

| # | Boundary | Why it is in character | How it could be tested | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **He will never falsify a measurement.** He will hide, delay, bury or classify data, but never alter what an instrument recorded. | His whole creed is that civilisation runs on true knowledge of physical systems; a lie inside the data is the one thing that makes collapse certain | A Compact member falsified records (as in the first case's backstory); the only way to save the empire is to keep the falsification standing, or to fabricate a counter-measurement | The line between "bury" and "falsify" must be crisp, or players will call it hypocrisy. That may be the point. |
| B2 | **He will never cause a radiological release on purpose**, nor allow nuclear technology to be used as a weapon | If nuclear power is ever seen as a weapon, public trust dies and with it the infrastructure he lives for | A rival or a rogue member threatens a release; preventing it requires exposing the Compact | Very close to "doesn't want mass casualties", which any non-cartoon villain shares; may not feel like a sacrifice |
| B3 | **He will never harm a child**, or allow the Compact to | Personal history (open: a lost child, his own childhood) | The only protection for the Compact is leverage over a child; or a child is the witness | Familiar from crime fiction; needs a specific backstory to avoid cliché |
| B4 | **He will never cut the public's essential services**, not for a day, not to win | It is the reason he exists; to do it would be to become what he fights | An opponent (or the player) makes exposure possible only at the cost of a brownout he could prevent by surrendering | Strong fit with the brief's central question (brief §8) |
| B5 | **He will never betray a person who has trusted him personally** | His loyalty is real; it is why members stay | The player has trusted him; protecting the Compact requires betraying that trust | Makes the player relationship the crux; a natural climax for "affinity is not morality" (brief §11) |
| B6 | **He will never take a life with his own hands or by his own direct order** | He allows deaths, tolerates them, profits from them, but has never said the word | Someone forces the choice: give the order, or lose everything | Morally evasive by design; the test is whether the player calls it what it is |

**Interaction with the first case.** In the recommended option for *The
Quiet Dose*, a Compact member kills to protect a falsified record. Vale's
reaction when he learns of it can be written to fit any of B1, B5 or B6, and
is a good place to reveal the boundary later.

## 4. Candidate founding histories of the Compact (none chosen)

All must satisfy brief §7: embedded in society, protected for many motives,
not omnipotent, not every crime ordered from the top.

| # | Founding | Sketch | Fits the first case how |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The Long Dark** | After a multi-week grid failure in the HTGR era (cause open: a storm, a cyber-attack, a cascading fuel-supply failure), a group of engineers, officials and financiers who kept the hospitals running made a private pact that it would never happen again, whatever it took. Vale was one of them, or their heir. | The 2158 cover-up is an early act "for stability": a release admitted then would have closed the station that powered the east of the city |
| F2 | **The Continuity Committee** | A lawful, secret civil-contingency committee, created to keep reactors and grids running during a war or crisis, which was never wound up, lost its legal mandate, and kept its members, methods and secrecy | Members can honestly believe they are still serving the state; strongest "ideological agreement" motive |
| F3 | **Vale's own creation** | Vale built it in his thirties by gathering people who shared a guilty secret, offering them protection in exchange for loyalty | The 2158 participants are recruited because of their guilt; strongest "shared responsibility for earlier wrongdoing" motive |
| F4 | **An inheritance** | A network that has existed in some form since the late nineteenth century, passed down his mother's line | Ties directly to Moriarty's organisation (text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L9211-L9235). **Risk:** makes the conspiracy hereditary, which pulls against "genealogy is not proof of guilt"; use only if the story then questions the inheritance |

## 5. Map of member types by motive and culpability

Columns are the brief's five motives (§7). Rows are culpability, high to
low. Each cell is an *archetype*, not a named character, except where the
first case supplies one (marked QD).

| Culpability ↓ / Motive → | Fear of retaliation or exposure | Ideological agreement | Economic and technological dependency | Shared responsibility for earlier wrongdoing | Loyalty and institutional self-preservation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High: commits violent crimes** | The cornered official who kills to avoid exposure | The true believer who regards a death as a fair price for stability | The fixer paid to make problems vanish | **QD: Julian Marsh** (recommended option): kills his oldest friend to keep a 31-year-old falsification buried | The enforcer who acts on "what Vale would want" |
| **Middle: falsifies, suppresses, obstructs** | The regulator who quietly drops an inspection | The think-tank author who writes the public case for consolidation | The supplier who cannot lose the contract | The retired engineer who stays silent | The department head who closes ranks |
| **Low: tolerates, benefits, looks away** | The witness who forgets | The idealist who never asks how things got done | The city councillor whose district's heat depends on one operator | The family that took the old settlement money | The junior who signs what is put in front of them |
| **Unwitting: serves without knowing** | n/a | The scholarship student who repeats Vale's views because they are persuasive | The ordinary worker whose job depends on the system | n/a | The loyal assistant |

**Design rules drawn from the map:**

1. Every case's culprit should sit in one cell, and the player should be able
   to see which, by the end of the case.
2. Members in the bottom two rows are sympathetic and often recruitable as
   allies; exposing them carelessly should cost the player.
3. Moving a character between cells over time (the junior who signed in 2158
   rising to the top row in 2189) is the strongest kind of reveal.

## 6. How members act without Vale's orders

Brief §7: members may act independently "to protect the system, their own
interests, or what they believe Vale wants." Proposed mechanisms, each of
which leaves a different kind of evidence for the player:

1. **No written orders, ever.** The Compact's culture forbids instructions in
   writing. That protects Vale and also means members routinely *guess*. Each
   guess is a chance for a crime Vale never wanted.
2. **Mutual holding of secrets.** Members know each other's compromises
   (cf. Milverton's trade in compromising letters,
   text:the-return-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L6226-L6232). Nobody leaves
   because everybody can expose everybody. This is the "shared
   responsibility" motive made structural.
3. **Anticipatory loyalty.** Members compete to solve problems before they
   reach Vale, because bringing him a problem is a sign of failure. The first
   case's culprit acts partly from this.
4. **Shields, not orders.** What the Compact provides most reliably is
   protection after the fact: lawyers, "lost" files, sympathetic
   appointments. Doyle's version: "money is found for his bail or his
   defence" (text:the-memoirs-of-sherlock-holmes.txt#L9231-L9232). A member
   who acts alone counts on the shield arriving.
5. **The shield is not automatic.** Vale decides afterwards whether to
   protect a member who acted alone. That decision, and how he makes it, is
   one of the best windows onto his character and his moral boundary.

## 7. What the Compact cannot do (to keep it from omnipotence)

- It cannot alter physics. A contamination that happened can be measured; a
  decay that started cannot be stopped. Measurement is the Compact's natural
  enemy (see world-bible §2.4).
- It cannot control every institution at once. It has people in the
  Directorate, in conglomerates, in courts, but never all of any of them.
  Honest people in each are the player's allies.
- It cannot act quickly without leaving traces. Its preference is for slow,
  deniable pressure; urgent crimes are committed by individuals, badly.
- It is not united. Factions (believers, exploiters, the trapped) want
  different things.

## 8. Vale in the first case (recommended option)

- He appears **before Elias's death**, opening a Vale Foundation clinic for
  decommissioning workers in Belvedere Flats; this is real charity.
- He **funds Dr Voss's independent laboratory**, which is what eventually
  makes the truth measurable. His charity enables the case against one of his
  own members. This should be allowed to stand as genuinely ironic, not as a
  hidden plan.
- He has **no knowledge of the killing in advance** in the recommended
  option. Whether he knew of the 2158 falsification is left open; the drafts
  write around it.
- At the end, he may contact the player. What he says depends on the open
  boundary choice; three sketches:
  - *(fits B1)* "Julian buried a measurement. I would have dug it up myself,
    in time, and in the right way."
  - *(fits B5)* "He was my friend for twenty years. I will pay for his
    defence, and I will not lift a finger to save him from the verdict."
  - *(fits B6)* "I never asked anyone to do anything of the kind." (True, and
    the player should be allowed to find it insufficient.)
